## D-01 ✅ CLOSED — Relationship to Abī Sulaimiy College and SH Royal Schools

> **RULED 2 August 2026 — Option D: umbrella group.** SH Royal Schools and Abī Sulaimiy
> College form an educational group; Al-Madinah International College is its tertiary and
> international arm. Incorporated at `EB §8.3`.
>
> **What this settles:** the structure. **What it does not settle:** the facts. Per
> `EB §46`, no group figure — founding year, student numbers, staff numbers, years of
> operation, registration standing — may appear on any public surface until it is
> evidenced and dated. The group relationship may be stated as a bare fact; nothing may be
> quantified around it yet.
>
> **Consequences raised:** **D-12** (group identity, naming, and endorsement model).
>
> **Still needed from the Founder, to convert this ruling into publishable substance:**
> the group's legal structure and registered name; each member institution's registration
> status and standing; real student and staff numbers with an `as of` date; which faculty
> and premises transfer to Al-Madinah; and written authority to speak on the group's
> behalf. These are the facts that retire most of `EB §46.3`.

**Original question.** Is Al-Madinah International College a successor to, a sibling of, a
rebrand of, or entirely independent from `abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` and SH Royal Schools?

**Why I cannot answer it.** Both `shroyalschools.com` and `abisulaimiycollege.com.ng` were
blocked at this environment's network gateway (403 on CONNECT). The `shroyalschools`
repository contains 17 photographs and no site code. Web search returned no substantive
record of Abī Sulaimiy College. Editorial Bible §46 forbids me from characterising an
institution I could not examine.

**Why it matters.** It determines whether Al-Madinah inherits an existing student body,
faculty, reputation, physical premises, and regulatory registration — or starts from zero.
That single fact changes the entire Phase 2 strategy, the Phase 7 admissions design, and
what §46.3's status register can be retired.

**Options.**
| | Model | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Successor** — Al-Madinah replaces Abī Sulaimiy | Inherits students, faculty, registration, reputation. Requires a migration and communication plan. Fastest route to a real, non-empty institution. |
| B | **Sibling** — both operate, different segments | Two brands to fund. Requires a clear, published boundary between them. |
| C | **Independent** — no relationship | Cleanest brand, but the §46.3 status register stays long and full. |
| D | **Umbrella** — SH Royal / Abī Sulaimiy as a group, Al-Madinah as its tertiary arm | Strongest institutional story if the group is real and in good standing. |

**Recommendation.** If Abī Sulaimiy College is an operating institution with real students
and staff, **Option A or D**. An institution with real history beats a beautiful empty
one, and it retires most of §46.3 immediately. If it is dormant or was itself a concept,
**Option C**.

**I need from you:** what these institutions actually are, whether they operate today,
their student and staff numbers, their registration status, and whether their assets and
people come with us.

**Blocks:** Phase 2 (Institutional Strategy).

## D-02 🔴 — Name collision with Al-Madinah International University (MEDIU)

**Question.** Do we keep the name "Al-Madinah International College" unchanged?

**The problem.** An established institution operates in Malaysia as **Al-Madinah
International University (MEDIU) / جامعة المدينة العالمية**. "Al-Madinah International
College" and "Al-Madinah International University" are close enough to create three
exposures: search and discovery contamination for years; potential trademark
challengeability in jurisdictions where MEDIU holds or claims marks in education
services; and confusion when a student or employer tries to verify our certificate.

I am flagging exposure, not giving a legal opinion. The trademark position in Nigeria,
the UK, the EU, and the GCC requires an attorney in each.

**Options.**
| | Option | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Add a distinguishing element** — e.g. *Al-Madinah International College of Arabic and Qur'anic Studies*, or a founder/place qualifier | Keeps the name and its meaning. Removes most SEO and most trademark exposure. Slightly longer. |
| B | **Keep unchanged**, take trademark advice, budget the SEO cost | Cleanest name. Accepts years of competing against a larger institution for our own name, plus residual legal risk. |
| C | **Reposition** to a distinctive Arabic name, "Madinah" as descriptor | Strongest differentiation. Loses the immediate recognition the current name buys. |

**Recommendation: Option A.** It preserves everything the name is meant to signal while
removing most of the collision, and it has the incidental benefit of stating what we
actually teach — which is better positioning anyway.

**Cost of deciding late.** Every day this is open, any spend on logo design, domains,
trademark filing, print, or signage is at risk. This is the cheapest decision to make now
and the most expensive to make in month nine.

**Blocks:** Phase 4 (Brand Identity). I will not commission or design a mark until this is
ruled on.

## D-03 🔴 — Legal use of "College" and award nomenclature, per jurisdiction

**Question.** May we lawfully call ourselves a "College" and issue "Diplomas" and
"Certificates" in each market we operate in?

**⚠ Escalated by the D-04 ruling.** The approved award ladder includes an **"Arabic
Language Diploma."** This decision is no longer theoretical — it now gates a named,
approved award. Until counsel clears the term in each market we market in, that award
cannot be printed, coded into the certificate system, or advertised. Fallback ready:
"Certificate of Mastery in Arabic — Level n."

**Why it matters.** Education nomenclature is regulated. In Nigeria, tertiary
nomenclature and award-granting sit under NUC/NBTE/TRCN frameworks depending on what is
being claimed. In the UK, "university" is protected and "college" carries expectations,
with the CMA applying consumer-protection law to higher-education marketing. Several US
states regulate the operation of degree-granting institutions serving their residents,
including online. Getting this wrong is not a branding problem, it is an enforcement
problem — and one that surfaces exactly when we are large enough to notice.

**What I recommend regardless of the ruling.**
1. **Never use "degree," "BA," "MA," "university," or "accredited"** until a competent
   regulator says we may. (Editorial Bible §36.3.)
2. Use **"Certificate of Completion"** and **"Certificate of Mastery"** as the default
   award names — descriptive, honest, and unregulated in most jurisdictions.
3. Take a written opinion in Nigeria first (home market), then the UK, before Phase 3
   finalises award names, and budget for it in Phase 8.

**Blocks:** Phase 3 (Academic Framework) and Phase 7 (Admissions). Award names cannot be
finalised without it.

## D-04 ✅ CLOSED — Do we issue ijāzah? Under whose authority?

> **RULED 2 August 2026 — Option B now, A later.** No ijāzah is issued until qualified
> teachers with authentic, verifiable chains join the institution. Incorporated at
> `EB §34.7` and `EB §36.2–36.3`.
>
> **The award ladder, as specified by the Founder:**
>
> | Award | Attests to |
> |---|---|
> | Qur'an Memorisation Certificate | Portion memorised **and retained** to a stated *itqān* grade |
> | Tajwīd Proficiency Certificate | Command of tajwīd in the declared riwāyah, demonstrated in recitation |
> | Arabic Language Diploma ⚠ | Mastery of a named Arabic level across all five modalities |
> | Certificate of Completion | Attendance and completion, with no mastery claim |
>
> Every award states on its face what it is **not** — specifically, that it is not an
> ijāzah and confers no chain of transmission. That sentence protects the student, the
> institution, and the tradition at once.
>
> ⚠ **One flag on the Founder's own wording.** "Diploma" is a **regulated term** in
> Nigeria, the UK, and parts of the EU and US — which is exactly D-03's scope, still open.
> The name is recorded as the Founder's intent and does **not** ship until counsel
> confirms it is lawful in each market where we market it. Clearable fallback:
> **"Certificate of Mastery in Arabic — Level n"**, which says the same thing and is
> unregulated. I have not renamed it unilaterally, and I have not let it through
> unflagged.
>
> **Conditions for ever issuing ijāzah** are now written down at `EB §36.3` — four of
> them, all published, all required. Writing the conditions now, while there is no
> commercial pressure, is the point: it is much harder to lower a published bar later than
> to set it honestly today.

**Original question.** Will Al-Madinah issue ijāzah in Qur'anic recitation, and if so,
through which teachers, in which riwāyah, with which chains?

**Why this is the highest integrity risk in the institution.** An ijāzah is a claim about
an unbroken chain of transmission. A fabricated, careless, or unverifiable ijāzah is not a
marketing error — it is a corruption of the thing we exist to protect. It would be the
single most damaging discoverable fact about us, and it would be permanent.

**Options.**
| | Option | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Issue ijāzah** through named teachers who hold one in that riwāyah, with published chains, after full recitation, recorded in a verifiable register | The strongest possible credential and a genuine differentiator. Requires real muqriʾīn on staff. |
| B | **Do not issue ijāzah.** Issue institutional certificates of mastery, and say plainly that we do not grant ijāzah | Completely honest, zero risk, and still valuable. |
| C | **Partner** with an institution or muqriʾ who does, and route eligible students to them | Credible bridge while we build capacity. |

**Recommendation: B now, A later, C as the bridge.** Publish plainly: *"We do not
currently grant ijāzah. Our certificates attest to mastery assessed against our published
standard. Students seeking ijāzah are referred to [named partners]."* Move to A only when
real muqriʾīn with verifiable chains are appointed. Editorial Bible §34.7 makes this
binding either way: **if we cannot do it properly, we do not do it, and we say so.**

**Blocks:** Phase 3. Also blocks any marketing that touches certification.

## D-05 🟡 — Published statement of academic method

**Question.** What does Al-Madinah publish about its ʿaqīdah, manhaj, and fiqh teaching?

**Why it matters, particularly in West Africa.** In our home market, an Islamic
institution's perceived alignment materially determines who enrols, who teaches, and who
recommends us. Silence is itself read as a position — and read differently by every
constituency. Ambiguity does not keep everyone; it loses everyone slowly.

**Recommendation.** Publish a short, dignified **Statement of Academic Method** that says:
which texts we teach from; which madhhab we teach for fiqh instruction (and that we teach
it as instruction, not as adjudication); that recognised differences among the schools are
taught with respect and not litigated; and that we issue no fatwā (Editorial Bible §5.7).
That is enough to let a family decide, without turning the institution into a partisan
one.

**This statement must be written by the Founder and named scholars, not by me.** I can
draft its structure; I cannot supply its content, and I will not.

**Blocks:** Phase 2.

## D-06 🟡 — Riwāyah taught and displayed

**Question.** Which riwāyah does the institution teach, display, and certify?

**Working assumption:** Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim, with the muṣḥaf al-Madīnah text — the
overwhelming standard in West Africa's formal institutions and globally.

**But:** Warsh ʿan Nāfiʿ is significant across parts of West and North Africa and may
matter for our positioning and for specific student communities. If we intend to teach
more than one, that decision affects the muṣḥaf typeface, the audio library, the
assessment rubrics, and the certificate wording — and is far cheaper to make now than to
retrofit.

**Recommendation.** Confirm Ḥafṣ as primary. State explicitly whether Warsh is offered.
Never mix riwāyāt on one surface without labelling (Editorial Bible §15.4).

**Blocks:** Phase 3 and Phase 6 (typeface and audio architecture).

## D-07 🟡 — Mixed-gender imagery and class policy

**Question.** What is our policy on mixed-gender classes, and on photography showing
adult men and women together?

**Why it matters.** It affects who enrols, how we staff, how we schedule, and every
photograph we ever commission. Deciding it after commissioning photography is expensive.

**Options.** Fully segregated · segregated for adults, mixed for children under a stated
age · mixed with a stated modesty standard · student-selectable (offer both, let the
student choose).

**Recommendation: student-selectable**, with segregated as the default and mixed
available on request, and a photography policy that shows single-gender contexts only.
This maximises reach without asking any family to compromise. Until this is ruled on, no
mixed adult classroom photography ships (Editorial Bible §5.4).

**Blocks:** Phase 4 (photography direction) and Phase 7 (scheduling architecture).

## D-09 🟡 — Free trial lesson

**Question.** Do we offer a free trial lesson with a real teacher, and what does it cost
us?

**The case for.** Our teachers are our strongest asset. In a category where every
competitor's copy sounds identical, meeting an actual teacher is the highest-converting
possible experience — and it is honest, which most high-converting tactics are not.

**The cost.** A real teacher hour per prospect, with a no-show rate that will be
significant in some markets.

**Recommendation.** Offer it, but **gate it behind the free placement assessment** — a
student who has completed a 10-minute assessment has demonstrated intent, which cuts
no-shows sharply, and arrives with a placement result that makes the trial lesson
genuinely useful rather than a sales call. Cap trials per teacher per week so faculty
workload stays protected (Editorial Bible §29).

**Blocks:** Phase 7 and the Phase 8 unit economics.

## D-10 ✅ CLOSED — Regional pricing: disclosed or undisclosed

> **RULED 2 August 2026 — disclosed.** Incorporated at `EB §38`.
>
> **Settled policy:** band determined by **billing country or declared country of
> residence**, with verification proportionate to the size of the differential; IP demoted
> to an initial display hint that never determines a charge; methodology published;
> multiple currencies supported; scholarships and financial aid alongside the bands, not
> inside them; compliance with each jurisdiction's pricing-transparency law.
>
> **Two refinements I have added under this ruling**, both flowing from it rather than
> from anything separately decided — flag either if you disagree:
>
> 1. **Currency is a separate axis from band** (`EB §38.2.7`). Band sets *what* a student
>    pays; currency sets *how*. A Band A student in Riyadh sees SAR, one in London sees
>    GBP, a Band D student in Lagos sees NGN — exactly the structure your ruling
>    describes. Keeping the two axes independent stops FX movement from silently
>    re-banding anyone.
> 2. **Price is locked for the duration of an enrolled programme** (`EB §38.2.8`). A
>    student holds the price they enrolled at through to completion, regardless of later
>    band revisions or FX. This is what makes an *annual, published* methodology review
>    safe to commit to — without it, every review would re-price existing students and the
>    transparency would become a liability rather than an asset.
>
> **A cost consequence to carry into Phase 8:** the ruling names **Niger and Chad** in
> Band E and Senegal in Band D — all Francophone. Pricing for those markets means
> marketing to them, and marketing to them in English does not work. **French
> localisation therefore moves up from expansion Phase 5 to a Phase 2–3 consideration**
> (`EB §38.3`, `EB §43`). Better to budget it now than to discover it after launch.

**Original question.** Do we publish our regional pricing bands, or apply them silently by
IP?

**This is the most consequential commercial decision in the register.** Full argument at
Editorial Bible §38.

**The brief asked for:** IP-based differential pricing applied automatically, "without
even announcing to students."

**My recommendation: keep the economics exactly as asked, publish the mechanism.**

| | Disclosed (recommended) | Undisclosed (as briefed) |
|---|---|---|
| **Economics** | Identical | Identical |
| **Legal exposure** | Low — disclosure is the standard defence | Engages consumer-protection law in the EU, UK, US, and Nigeria. Requires counsel in each market. |
| **If discovered** | Nothing to discover | A trust collapse at an institution whose product *is* trust |
| **Accuracy** | Billing-country verified — accurate | IP alone — misidentifies diaspora, VPN, corporate, and roaming students |
| **VPN arbitrage** | Blocked at payment by billing country | **Trivially defeated** — free VPN gets the cheap price |
| **Marketing value** | A published methodology becomes a *reason to trust us* | None; it is a liability held quietly |
| **Consistency with §3.1, §6.4** | Compliant | In conflict — this is *ghish* |

Note the fourth and fifth rows in particular: the undisclosed version is not merely
riskier, **it works less well at the thing it is for.** IP-only detection hands the cheap
price to whoever installs a VPN and the expensive price to a Nigerian student on a proxied
mobile carrier. Billing-country verification is both more defensible *and* more accurate.

**The fair counter-argument.** Disclosed differentials invite deliberate arbitrage; some
Band A students may perceive them as unfair to themselves; and competitors in this
category do it silently. My assessment is that billing-country verification handles the
first, the trust gained from every other student outweighs the second, and the third is
not a standard this institution should adopt.

**But this is your call.** I will implement whatever you rule. Editorial Bible §46 obliges
me to record that I advised against the undisclosed variant and to have set out why —
that record protects you as much as it protects me.

**Blocks:** Phase 8 (Pricing) and Phase 9 (checkout architecture).

## D-11 🟢 — Do we accept zakāt?

**Question.** Will the scholarship fund accept zakāt, and can we administer it properly?

**The requirement if yes.** Ring-fenced accounts, disbursement only to eligible
recipients under a published fiqh position from a named scholar, separate accounting,
independent audit, and public reporting. Zakāt mixed into operating revenue is a serious
failure — financial and religious at once.

**Recommendation.** **Not in year one.** Accept general sadaqah and named scholarship
sponsorship, both audited and publicly reported. Introduce zakāt only once there is a
named scholar governing eligibility and an independent auditor. Editorial Bible §40.3 is
binding: if we cannot administer it to that standard, we do not accept it.

**Blocks:** Phase 8, scholarship funding model.

## D-12 🟡 — Group identity, naming, and endorsement model *(raised by the D-01 ruling)*

**Question.** Now that Al-Madinah is the tertiary arm of an umbrella group, what is the
group called, does it have its own mark, and how much of the parent appears on
Al-Madinah's surfaces?

**Why this arises.** D-01 settled that there *is* a group. It did not settle what the
group is called or how visible it is. Every letterhead, footer, certificate, and legal
notice needs an answer, and getting it wrong in either direction is costly: too little
parent presence wastes the credibility the umbrella was chosen for; too much makes an
international brand carry a domestic parent's recognition burden.

**Options.**
| | Model | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Light-touch endorsement** — Al-Madinah leads; a small, consistent group line in the footer and on formal documents | International brand stands on its own; parent supplies credibility on demand. Standard for university groups. |
| B | **Co-equal dual lockup** — both marks, everywhere | Maximum inherited credibility in Nigeria; dilutes Al-Madinah abroad, where the parent means nothing. |
| C | **Silent parent** — group exists legally, is not surfaced in branding | Cleanest international brand; forfeits the entire benefit of the umbrella ruling. |

**Recommendation: Option A.** It captures the credibility the umbrella was chosen for
without spending Al-Madinah's recognition on it. Concretely: an endorsement line
("A member of [group]") in the footer, on letterhead, on formal correspondence, and on
certificates — never in the header, never in the logo lockup, never in advertising.

**I also need to know:** does the group have a registered name today, or does one need
creating? If one needs creating, it should be decided **alongside D-02**, not after —
they are the same design and trademark exercise and should be run once, not twice.

**Blocks:** Phase 4, jointly with D-02.
