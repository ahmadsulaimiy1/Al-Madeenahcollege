# PART I — INSTITUTIONAL EDITORIAL FOUNDATION

## §1. Identity

**Arabic name.** كلية المدينة العالمية للدراسات العربية والإسلامية **[I]**

**English name.** Al-Madeenah International College for Arabic & Islamic Studies **[I]**

**Short forms.** *Al-Madeenah* (English) · *كلية المدينة* (Arabic). Never "AMICAIS" — an
institution that needs an acronym has a name problem, not an abbreviation problem.

### §1.1 What changed, and why it is recorded

This supersedes the previously ratified *Al-Madinah International College of Arabic and
Qur'anic Studies*. Three changes, each deliberate:

| Was | Is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Al-Mad**i**nah | Al-Mad**ee**nah | Founder's orthography. The doubled vowel carries the long *ī* of المدينة for a reader with no Arabic, which the single *i* does not. |
| الدولية | العالمية | الدولية reads as *inter-state*, the register of treaties and governments. العالمية is *of the world* — the correct word for an institution whose students share no state. |
| of Arabic and **Qur'anic** Studies | for Arabic & **Islamic** Studies | The narrower name misdescribed the College. Qur'an is one of three schools, not the whole. The Qur'an loses nothing by being named accurately as part of a larger body. |

**[V] Legal.** The registered name is a Founder and legal matter. Nothing here asserts
registration. See `D-13`.

## §2. Purpose

To make serious Arabic and Islamic learning reachable by anyone with a phone and an
intention, without lowering what "serious" means. **[I]**

## §3. Vision

**[I]** A college without walls that a student in Lagos, London, Dakar, Riyadh or Paris
enters on the same terms, studies at a pace their life permits, and leaves with a record
another institution can evaluate.

## §4. Mission

**[I]** To teach Arabic as the instrument that opens the tradition; to teach the Qur'an as
something *retained* rather than merely reached; to teach the Islamic sciences from named
texts with honest attribution; and to certify only what we can evidence.

## §5. The educational promise

Four sentences. Every one is falsifiable, which is the point.

1. **What you are taught is fixed. When you learn it is yours.** The standard does not
   move because a student is fast, slow, employed, or poor.
2. **You advance because you demonstrated something, not because time passed.**
3. **You will never be charged to prove that you studied here.** Verification of your own
   credential is free and permanent.
4. **The price of the education is not the price of the dignity.** Service tiers differ.
   Academic standing does not.

## §6. Founder principles carried forward

From the Founder's Master Roadmap, which remains the origin document of this institution
and is treated as historical founder material rather than operative policy:

| Principle | Standing |
|---|---|
| Affordable premium Arabic and Qur'anic education | **[I]** Carried forward intact |
| The institution must be financially sustainable, not merely enrolled | **[I]** Carried forward and strengthened (`§6.1`) |
| The institution must outlast its founder | **[I]** Carried forward; drives `§59` archives |
| A four-month online programme, Standard/Advanced/Premium at ₦6,000/₦8,000/₦10,000 | **[Superseded]** — see `§6.1` and `D-17` |
| Separate registration, ID, certificate and transcript charges | **[Superseded]** — see `§6.1` |

### §6.1 The financing correction

The Roadmap's instinct — that the institution must be sustainable — was right and is
retained. Its *mechanism* is corrected, on the Founder's own instruction.

**Credentials will not finance the College.** Charging a student to obtain evidence of
their own completed education creates an incentive the institution must never hold: it
makes the College better off when a graduate needs proof of their own study. That is a
structural conflict, not a pricing preference.

**The model is cross-subsidy** **[I]**:

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| Core tuition | Highly affordable. Covers direct teaching cost, not the whole institution. |
| Premium service tiers | Higher margin. Buys *service*, never standing (`§45`). |
| Specialist and corporate programmes | Higher margin. Subsidises core. |
| Optional private tutoring | Optional. Never required to pass. |
| Donations, waqf, endowment | Ring-fenced to the Access Fund. |
| Administrative credential services | **Transparent cost recovery only.** Never a margin line. |

**Binding rule.** The first issue of every certificate and every transcript is free. Only
*replacement*, *expedited handling* and *physical production and postage* may be charged,
at documented cost. Verification is free forever, to everyone. **[I]**

Full unit economics belong to the *Institutional Master Plan*, the next document in
sequence (`§65`). This Bible fixes the principles that model must satisfy.

2. The College issues no fatwā and takes no position in recognised scholarly

## §9. Academic integrity

Integrity is symmetrical: it binds the institution before it binds the student.

**On the institution:** we do not award what we cannot assess; we do not name a level we
cannot define; we do not describe a pathway we have never run as though we had.

**On the student:** work submitted is the student's own; assistance is declared;
impersonation in an assessment voids the attempt and is recorded permanently.

**On the assistant:** `§34`.

## §10. The institutional honesty protocol

`EB §46` is imported whole and is not subordinate to any provision of this Bible.

Its operative consequence for academic writing: **no page may describe a thing the College
has not done as though it had done it.** We have admitted no students. Therefore no
graduation figure, no completion rate, no testimonial, no "our students", no alumni claim,
and no faculty biography may appear until each is true and dated. Where a page needs to
describe a capability we have built but not yet used, it says so in those words.

# PART II — ACADEMIC PHILOSOPHY

## §13. What education is for, here

Four outcomes, ranked. When they conflict, the higher governs.

1. **Access to the tradition.** A graduate can open a classical text and make progress in
   it without a translator standing between them and it.
2. **Retention.** What was learned is still there a year later. An education that
   evaporates was not an education.
3. **Character (adab).** The manner of seeking knowledge is part of the knowledge.
4. **Independence.** The graduate can continue without us. An institution that makes
   itself necessary forever has failed.

## §14. The governing formula

> **Fixed standards + flexible pace + mastery-based progression + differentiated service.**

Each clause carries weight, and each constrains the others:

- **Fixed standards** — the syllabus, the gates and the evidence required are identical
  for every student on every pathway and at every price.
- **Flexible pace** — the *calendar* is the student's. Employment, family, Ramadan,
  travel and bandwidth are real, and an institution that ignores them is not serious
  about access.
- **Mastery-based progression** — the student advances on demonstrated competence.
- **Differentiated service** — money buys attention, convenience and access to more
  teaching. It never buys a grade, a gate, a level or a credential.

## §15. Why mastery, and what it costs us

Time-based progression fails in both directions: it graduates a student who paid for
nine months and learned little, and it holds back a student who mastered the material in
three. Both are injustices, and the second is the one poor students suffer most, because
they cannot afford to buy time they do not need.

**What mastery costs the College, stated plainly:** unpredictable revenue timing,
higher assessment load, and the operational difficulty of a cohort whose members are at
different points. These are accepted. They are the price of the claim.

## §16. Mastery defined

A student has demonstrated mastery of a unit when, **assessed by a qualified human**, they
can perform its stated competencies **to the stated criterion**, **on demand**, **without
notice**, **after an interval**.

Five clauses, and every one is load-bearing:

| Clause | What it excludes |
|---|---|
| Assessed by a qualified human | An auto-marked quiz alone can never constitute a gate |
| Stated competencies | "Did the coursework" |
| To the stated criterion | A pass mark set after the fact |
| On demand | A rehearsed performance |
| Without notice, after an interval | Cramming — the thing that makes ḥifẓ evaporate |

### §16.1 The Retention Rule

**A portion of the Qur'an, or a competency in Arabic, counts as *held* only if it survives
unannounced re-testing after a defined interval.** Most institutions record what a student
*reached*. Al-Madeenah records what a student *retains*. **[Binding]**

This is the single strongest academic claim the College makes, and it is the one most
easily faked. It is therefore the one most tightly evidenced: every retention test is
timestamped, recorded, and attributable to a named assessor in the student record
(`§40`).

## §17. What a failed gate means

A failed gate is a diagnostic, not a verdict. It produces a specific statement of what was
missing and a targeted remediation plan.

**Retakes are unlimited and free.** **[Binding]** A College that charges for a retake has
made a student's difficulty into a revenue line, and has given itself a reason to prefer
failure. There is no version of that which survives `§9`.

## §18. What the College will not claim

It will not claim that distance learning is equivalent to sitting with a teacher.
It is not. It is *reachable*, which for the great majority of our students is the
difference between this education and no education at all. That is a sufficient claim and
it has the advantage of being true.

# PART III — THE ACADEMIC ARCHITECTURE

## §19. Levels are not durations

**[R][Binding]** This resolves the central ambiguity in the founding material.

A *level* is a defined body of competence, measured in **notional learning hours (NLH)**
and gated by demonstrated mastery. A *duration* is what happens when a particular student
applies a particular weekly pace to that body. **The level is fixed. The months are an
output.**

The Founder's working figure — *Level 100 ≈ two months* — is **arithmetically correct as
one of four pathways**, and the modelling below shows exactly which one. It was never
wrong; it was a pace being read as a definition.

### §19.1 The unit: notional learning hours

**1 NLH = one hour of a typical student's total effort** — live sessions, recorded
lessons, reading, drill, memorisation, murājaʿah, assignments and assessment combined.

NLH is used rather than "credits" deliberately. *Credit unit*, *credit hour* and
*semester hour* carry defined regulatory meanings in Nigeria and elsewhere, and using them
would imply a recognition the College does not hold (`§62`). NLH states the same
information without borrowing an authority we have not earned.

**[V] Comparability note, not an equivalence claim.** European Credit Transfer System
practice treats 1 ECTS as 25–30 notional hours. A reader may divide our NLH figures by 25
to obtain an indicative comparison. This is offered so a foreign registrar can orient
themselves. It is **not** a claim of ECTS recognition, and no College surface may present
it as one.

## §20. The Arabic ladder

Four levels. The hours are derived, not chosen — the derivation is in `§20.2` so that any
reviewer can check it.

| Level | Arabic | NLH | Cumulative | Indicative CEFR **[V]** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **100** Foundation | التمهيدي | 200 | 200 | ~A1 |
| **200** Intermediate | المتوسّط | 300 | 500 | ~A2 |
| **300** Advanced | المتقدّم | 450 | 950 | ~B1 |
| **400** Mastery | الإتقان | 600 | 1,550 | ~B2, weighted to the written and classical register |

**These are internal academic levels.** They are not a degree, not degree-equivalent, and
carry no implication of one, unless and until an accreditation framework says otherwise
(`§62`). The numbering follows familiar convention only so that a student and a foreign
registrar can read the ladder at a glance.

### §20.1 What each level actually means

| Level | The student can, on demand |
|---|---|
| **100** | Read fully vowelled Arabic aloud accurately; hold a simple exchange about themselves and daily matters; write short vowelled sentences; recognise the three-letter root and the basic ismiyyah/fiʿliyyah distinction. |
| **200** | Read unvowelled simple prose with a lexicon; sustain conversation on familiar topics; write a connected paragraph; perform iʿrāb on a simple sentence; recognise the common ṣarf patterns. |
| **300** | Read graded classical text with a lexicon and derive meaning independently; follow a lecture delivered in Arabic; write structured expository prose; handle the major naḥw chapters; begin balāghah. |
| **400** | Read unedited classical text in the disciplines studied; follow and take part in scholarly discourse; write academic Arabic; translate accurately in both directions; use the classical lexica (Lisān, Qāmūs) as working tools. |

### §20.2 Where the hours come from

Published guided-learning-hour ranges for the Common European Framework describe roughly
**500–600 cumulative hours to B2** for a *Category I* language (French, Spanish) for an
English-speaking learner. The United States Foreign Service Institute classifies Arabic as
**Category IV — "super-hard"**, requiring on the order of **2,200 class hours** to reach
professional working proficiency against roughly **600–750** for Category I: a multiplier
in the region of **2.6–3.0×**.

Applying the lower, more conservative end of that multiplier to the B2 figure:

> 500–600 hours × 2.6 ≈ **1,300–1,560 NLH**

Our cumulative figure to Level 400 is **1,550 NLH**. The ladder is calibrated to the
conservative end of published Arabic difficulty research rather than to a number that
would make the programme look faster. **[V] — the calibration should be reviewed against
our own first-cohort completion data once it exists, and revised if the data disagrees.**

**Why Level 400 is scoped to the written and classical register.** Full FSI-style
professional proficiency includes dialectal fluency and negotiation-level speech, which
this College does not teach and should not imply. Our terminal target is *access to the
tradition in writing and scholarly speech*. Narrower, honestly stated, and achievable.

### §20.3 Pace, and the resolution of the two-month question

| Pathway | Weekly commitment | Level 100 (200 NLH) | Level 200 (300 NLH) | Full ladder (1,550 NLH) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accelerated** | ~25 h | **8 weeks ≈ 2 months** | 12 weeks ≈ 3 months | ~62 weeks ≈ 1 yr 3 mo |
| **Intensive** | ~15 h | 13 weeks ≈ 3 months | 20 weeks ≈ 5 months | ~103 weeks ≈ 2 yrs |
| **Regular** | ~8 h | 25 weeks ≈ 6 months | 38 weeks ≈ 9 months | ~194 weeks ≈ 3 yrs 9 mo |
| **Flexible** | ~4 h | 50 weeks ≈ 12 months | 75 weeks ≈ 17 months | capped — see `§22` |

**The Founder's figure is confirmed.** Level 100 in two months is exactly right *at the
accelerated pace of about twenty-five hours a week* — roughly a full-time commitment. It
is a real pathway and the College will offer it. What it is not is the definition of the
level, and it is not what a working adult with children should be sold.

**Accelerated is not a discount on the syllabus.** Identical hours, identical gates,
identical evidence — compressed into fewer weeks. `§21` makes that binding.

## §21. The equal-standard rule

**[Binding]** Across every pathway of a given programme, these are identical: total NLH,
learning outcomes, assessment instruments, mastery criteria, and the wording of the
credential awarded. **Only the calendar differs.**

A pathway may never be created by removing content. If a route cannot carry the full
hours, it is not a faster route — it is a different, shorter programme, and it must be
named and credentialed as one.

## §22. Flexibility without chaos

Flexibility that never ends is not flexibility; it is an abandoned enrolment that the
institution has not had the honesty to close. Every programme therefore carries:

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum sustained pace | 3 NLH/week averaged over any 8-week window |
| Maximum registration period | 2.5 × the regular-pathway duration |
| Approved leave | Up to 12 months, in blocks, on request, without penalty — the clock stops |
| Dormancy | 16 weeks below minimum pace with no approved leave → contact, then dormant status |
| Re-entry | Permitted. Assessed for currency; mastery already demonstrated is not re-tested unless the interval makes it unsafe |
| Lapse | At maximum registration, the enrolment closes and a Statement of Results is issued for what *was* completed |

**A student who runs out of time still receives a truthful record of what they achieved.**
Nothing is confiscated for lateness.

## §23. The school ladder — and a correction

The classical stages **Ibtidāʾiyyah**, **Iʿdādiyyah** and **Thanawiyyah** are carried
forward. But the Mandate's proposal — regular 3 years, intensive 2, accelerated 1, for all
three — contains a problem I am obliged to raise rather than implement.

### §23.1 The problem

These three names do not denote only a body of knowledge. In Nigeria and across the
Arabic-Islamic education world they denote **school stages** — primary, preparatory and
secondary — and secondary-stage certification is examined and awarded by external bodies.
In Nigeria the relevant body is the **National Board for Arabic and Islamic Studies
(NBAIS)**. **[F]** Such bodies characteristically specify minimum attendance duration for
a school-leaving award, precisely because a school-leaving certificate makes a claim about
a period of formation, not only about knowledge held. **[V] — NBAIS's current
requirements must be confirmed directly; nothing here should be relied on as a statement
of them.**

A one-year "accelerated Thanawiyyah" offered to a sixteen-year-old as a school-leaving
qualification would therefore be, at best, unrecognised, and at worst a
misrepresentation. **`§9` does not permit us to sell it.**

### §23.2 The correction: two routes, named differently

| | **Academic Route** | **Certificated School Route** |
|---|---|---|
| For | Adults and independent learners seeking the knowledge | Candidates seeking an externally recognised school-stage certificate |
| Paces | All four, including accelerated | The external body's duration rules govern |
| Gated by | Al-Madeenah mastery gates | External syllabus and external examination |
| Award | Al-Madeenah Certificate of Mastery, stage-named | The external award, with an Al-Madeenah record of preparation |
| Status | **[I]** Buildable now | **[V]** Blocked pending `D-14` |

This preserves the Founder's flexibility for the people it was meant for — adults
returning to serious study, who are the majority of a distance institution's intake — and
withholds it only where an external body's rules, not ours, are the binding constraint.

### §23.3 The stages

| Stage | Arabic | NLH | Regular | Intensive | Accelerated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ibtidāʾiyyah** | الابتدائية | 960 | 3 yrs @ ~8 h/wk | 2 yrs @ ~12 h/wk | 1 yr @ ~24 h/wk |
| **Iʿdādiyyah** | الإعدادية | 1,080 | 3 yrs @ ~9 h/wk | 2 yrs @ ~14 h/wk | 1 yr @ ~27 h/wk |
| **Thanawiyyah** | الثانوية | 1,200 | 3 yrs @ ~10 h/wk | 2 yrs @ ~15 h/wk | 1 yr @ ~30 h/wk |

Computed on a 40-week teaching year. The accelerated column is a genuine full-time
commitment and is described as such wherever it is offered; it is available on the
Academic Route only (`§23.2`).

## §24. The Qur'an ladder

Memorisation is not measured in hours; it is measured in **portions retained**. Hours are
recorded for planning and for the transcript's learning-volume column, not as the standard.

| Unit | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Portion** | One juzʾ (جزء) |
| **Indicative volume** | 60–80 NLH per juzʾ *including* murājaʿah to criterion **[R]** |
| **Full Qur'an** | 30 portions ≈ 1,800–2,400 NLH |
| **Standard** | Itqān grade + the Retention Rule (`§16.1`) |

The Qur'an ladder runs independently of the Arabic ladder and a student may be at Level
200 Arabic and hold twelve portions, or the reverse. Forcing them into one sequence would
be an administrative convenience purchased with academic sense.

**[Binding] No ijāzah.** `§37`.

# PART IV — CURRICULUM

## §25. What earns a place

**[Binding]** A subject enters the curriculum only if it passes all four tests:

1. **Necessity** — a graduate who lacks it cannot meet the level's stated outcomes.
2. **Teachability at distance** — we can actually teach and assess it remotely, well.
3. **Assessability** — we can evidence it. A subject we cannot assess is a subject we
   cannot certify, and therefore a subject we should not charge for.
4. **Capacity** — a qualified teacher exists, or can be appointed, to teach it.

A subject that fails any test is an **elective**, a **future possibility**, or absent. It
is never included to make the catalogue look substantial. `§64`.

## §26. The three schools

### §26.1 School of Arabic Language — كلية اللغة العربية

| Core (every student, every level) | Elective / later level |
|---|---|
| Reading (القراءة) | Literature (الأدب) — L300+ |
| Writing (الكتابة) | Balāghah (البلاغة) — L300+ |
| Listening (الاستماع) | Translation (الترجمة) — L300+ |
| Speaking & conversation (المحادثة) | Academic Arabic (العربية الأكاديمية) — L400 |
| Vocabulary (المفردات) | Research skills (مهارات البحث) — L400 |
| Naḥw (النحو) | Dictation (الإملاء) — folded into Writing, not standalone |
| Ṣarf (الصرف) | |
| Composition (الإنشاء) | |
| Qur'anic & classical register (العربية القرآنية والتراثية) — from L200 | |

**Decision recorded:** *Dictation* does not survive `§25.1` as a standalone subject at
distance; it is an assessment instrument within Writing. *Classical Arabic*, *Qur'anic
Arabic* and *Hadith Arabic* are not three subjects — they are one competence, **the
classical register**, taught with texts drawn from all three sources. Listing them
separately would triple the catalogue and teach nothing extra.

**Assessed across five modalities at every level:** reading, listening, speaking, writing,
and comprehension of classical text. The fifth is where conversational programmes have
nothing to offer, and it is not optional here.

### §26.2 School of the Qur'an — كلية القرآن

| Core | Elective / later |
|---|---|
| Nazrah (النظرة) — accurate reading from the muṣḥaf | Qurʾanic vocabulary (مفردات القرآن) |
| Tajwīd theory (التجويد) | Waqf and Ibtidāʾ (الوقف والابتداء) — advanced |
| Makhārij and Ṣifāt (المخارج والصفات) | |
| Recitation to criterion (التلاوة) | |
| Ḥifẓ (الحفظ) | |
| Murājaʿah (المراجعة) — **compulsory, scheduled, never optional** | |

**Murājaʿah is the stage most institutions skip and the one most responsible for ḥifẓ
loss.** Here it is compulsory and system-scheduled (`§29.1`).

**[V] The riwāyah must be declared.** Every Qur'an credential states the riwāyah in which
it was assessed. Until the Founder and a qualified muqriʾ settle this, no Qur'an award may
be issued. `D-06`.

### §26.3 School of Islamic Sciences — كلية العلوم الشرعية

| Core | Elective / later | Deliberately excluded now |
|---|---|---|
| ʿAqīdah (العقيدة) | Uṣūl al-fiqh (أصول الفقه) — advanced | Fatwā, in any form |
| Fiqh al-ʿibādāt (فقه العبادات) | Muṣṭalaḥ al-ḥadīth (مصطلح الحديث) — advanced | Contemporary rulings |
| Sīrah (السيرة) | Islamic history (التاريخ الإسلامي) | Comparative polemics |
| Ḥadīth — named collections, with attribution | Tafsīr (التفسير) — from L300, never before the Arabic supports it | |
| Adab and akhlāq (الآداب والأخلاق) | | |

**Tafsīr is gated behind Level 300 Arabic** **[R]** — deliberately, and at the cost of
enrolment. Tafsīr taught in translation to a student who cannot read the āyah is not
tafsīr; it is a summary of someone else's tafsīr. The College will not describe that as
the study of Qur'anic exegesis.

**"Contemporary issues"** is excluded from the core because the honest version of it is
fatwā-adjacent and `§8.2` forbids the College a position. It may return as a *seminar
series with named external scholars speaking in their own names*. **[P]**

## §27. Distance pedagogy

**[Binding]** We do not film a classroom. The two modes are designed against each other,
not stacked.

| **Live (synchronous)** — for what needs a human in the moment | **Flexible (asynchronous)** — for what needs repetition |
|---|---|
| Recitation and correction | Recorded lessons |
| Speaking and pronunciation | Reading and text study |
| Oral assessment and gates | Drill and exercises |
| Tutorials, difficulty, error diagnosis | Vocabulary and murājaʿah |
| Office hours, advising | Assignments, quizzes |
| Seminars | Independent study |

**The allocation rule:** if a competent adult could acquire it alone with good materials,
it is asynchronous. Live time is scarce and expensive, and it is reserved for what only a
teacher can do — *hear the error and name it*.

### §27.1 Time-zone architecture

**[Binding]** Student local time ≠ institution time ≠ teacher time. Every time displayed
to a student is rendered in that student's own timezone, always, with no exception and no
setting to get wrong.

One course. Multiple scheduled **sections** where demand justifies it — West Africa,
Europe, Gulf, Americas. Not multiple courses, not multiple systems, not different
syllabi. `§64`.

**No live session is scheduled across a student section's local Maghrib window.** **[I]**

### §27.2 The mobile and bandwidth covenant

`EB §48` is imported. Its academic consequences are binding on curriculum design, not only
on the interface:

1. **Audio-first is a design default, not a fallback.** Recitation, dictation, listening
   and vocabulary drill are authored as audio that stands alone. Video is an enhancement.
2. **Every lesson has a downloadable form** that works with the connection off.
3. **No assessment may require sustained live video.** Where video is genuinely required
   — identity confirmation, some oral gates — it is a *short*, scheduled, low-resolution
   segment, with an audio-only fallback path recorded as an approved accommodation.
4. **Total weight of a lesson's required assets is budgeted and published** to the
   student in megabytes before they open it. Data costs money. Pretending otherwise is a
   design decision made by someone who has never paid for it.
5. **Progress survives interruption.** A dropped connection never costs submitted work.

## §29. Institutional learning methods

**[R]** The Mandate invites signature methodologies. Restraint applies: a method earns a
name only when the *name itself* is what a student uses to recall the discipline. Three
qualify. The rest are taught under their classical names, because a 1,200-year-old
discipline does not need our branding, and dressing iʿrāb in a new acronym would be the
opposite of scholarly.

### §29.1 The Five Returns — المراجعات الخمس

The classical ḥifẓ cycle already distinguishes **sabaq** (today's new portion), **sabqi**
(recently memorised, still fragile) and **manzil** (the consolidated corpus). The
weakness in practice is that *sabqi* is left to the student's discretion, and that is
where memorisation is lost.

The Five Returns fixes the sabqi phase to an expanding retrieval schedule:

| Return | Interval after first mastery |
|---|---|
| First | 1 day |
| Second | 3 days |
| Third | 7 days |
| Fourth | 21 days |
| Fifth | 60 days |

A portion that survives all five joins the **manzil** — the perpetual cycle — and is
thereafter sampled without notice under `§16.1`. A portion that fails any return re-enters
at the first.

Expanding intervals are used rather than fixed ones because retrieval that is *just* hard
enough is what consolidates memory; a portion reviewed while still effortless teaches the
student very little. The schedule is generated by the system, not left to intention.

### §29.2 Root Families — الأسر الجذرية

Arabic is a root language, and vocabulary taught as an alphabetical list fights that
structure instead of using it. Vocabulary is introduced by **triliteral root family**: the
root, its common derived forms, the pattern that produces each, and the semantic thread
connecting them. A student who has met **ك-ت-ب** as a family has not learned one word;
they have learned a mechanism that will recognise the next forty.

### §29.3 The Retention Rule

`§16.1`. Named because it is the standard, and a standard that is not named is not
enforced.

# PART V — ASSESSMENT

## §30. Assessment philosophy

The sequence is **learn → practise → assess → demonstrate → progress**, and the last two
are separate on purpose. *Assessment* is frequent, cheap, and often automatic; it tells a
student where they are. *Demonstration* is a gate: infrequent, human, unannounced, and
recorded.

**[Binding] An automatically marked instrument may never constitute a mastery gate.** It
may inform one. It may never be one.

## §31. The instruments

| Instrument | Purpose | Marked by | Gate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic / placement | Where to start | System + human review | No |
| Quiz | Retrieval practice | Auto | No |
| Assignment | Application | Teacher | No |
| Written examination | Consolidated knowledge | Teacher, moderated | Yes |
| **Oral examination** | Speaking, reasoning, iʿrāb | Teacher, live | **Yes** |
| **Recitation assessment** | Tajwīd, accuracy, fluency | Qualified assessor, live | **Yes** |
| **Memorisation check** | Ḥifẓ and retention | Qualified assessor, unannounced | **Yes** |
| Listening / reading test | Receptive competence | Auto + human review | Contributory |
| Project | Extended independent work | Teacher, moderated | L300+ only |

## §32. Placement

Free, untimed, resumable, unlimited retakes, and **never presented as a score**. It exists
to answer the question that stops most people applying — *where would I even start, and
will I be embarrassed?* An instrument that answers that question with a mark has failed at
its only job.

## §33. Integrity at distance

Distance assessment has a real vulnerability and pretending otherwise would fail `§9`.
The College's answer is **design, not surveillance**:

1. **Weight the gates toward live oral assessment**, which is the hardest thing in
   education to fake and the easiest thing to do well remotely.
2. **Identity is confirmed at the gate**, by a human who has taught or assessed the
   student before.
3. **Unannounced re-testing** (`§16.1`) makes a bought performance worthless, because it
   would have to be bought repeatedly and indefinitely.
4. **Question banks rotate**; no gate uses a fixed paper.
5. **No invasive proctoring software.** **[Binding]** No webcam-monitoring, no
   screen-recording, no keystroke analysis, no browser lockdown. These fail
   disproportionately on cheap devices and poor connections — they would punish exactly
   the students this College exists for — and they buy less integrity than a five-minute
   oral does.

## §34. Moderation and appeal

**Moderation.** Every summative gate is sampled — minimum 10% or five scripts, whichever
is greater — by a second qualified assessor. Disagreement beyond one band triggers full
re-marking of the cohort.

**Appeal.** A student may appeal any gate outcome within 21 days. The appeal is heard by
someone who was not involved in the original decision. **Appeals are free.** The outcome
is written, reasoned, and recorded. A student may not appeal *academic judgement* alone —
they may appeal procedure, evidence, bias, and the application of published criteria.

## §35. Grading

**[R]** A four-band criterion-referenced scale, published with descriptors before the
assessment, and never norm-referenced:

| Band | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Itqān** | إتقان | Mastery, comfortably and consistently |
| **Jayyid** | جيّد | Meets the standard |
| **Maqbūl** | مقبول | Meets the minimum; specific weaknesses named |
| **Lam yablugh** | لم يبلغ | Not yet — with a diagnostic and a plan (`§17`) |

Never a percentage on a certificate. A percentage implies a precision that human
assessment of language and recitation does not have, and invites comparison between
students, which `§14` exists to prevent.

## §36. Supervised distance assessment

Where a gate requires supervision it is scheduled, live, human, and short. The supervisor
is a member of the College. **[Binding]** The College will not require a student to travel
to a physical centre, because `§12` would be a lie if it did.

### §37.1 Forbidden terminology

**[Binding][V]** The words **Diploma**, **Degree**, **Bachelor**, **Master**, **Doctorate**,
**Undergraduate**, **Postgraduate**, **Faculty of** (in the legal sense), and **University**
do not appear on any Al-Madeenah credential, page, advertisement or assistant reply, in any

attainment, not a borrowed academic rank. The distinction is deliberate and must be

### §37.2 Ijāzah

**[Binding]** The College does not grant ijāzah. Every Qur'an credential states on its face
that it is not an ijāzah and confers no chain of transmission. Students seeking one are
told plainly that we do not offer it and are referred onward.

Ijāzah becomes possible only when **all** of the following are true and evidenced:
qualified **muqriʾūn** are appointed; their own asānīd are verified by a competent
authority; the riwāyah is declared; an assessment methodology exists in writing; and
scholarly governance is constituted to oversee it. Until every one holds, the word does
not appear. `D-06`.

## §38. What every credential must state

Name of holder · credential type · programme · level · **notional learning hours** ·
competencies attested · riwāyah where applicable · grade band · date of award · **unique
credential ID** · the verification URL · and — where relevant — **an explicit statement of
what the credential does *not* certify.**

The last field is unusual and deliberate. A credential that is silent about its limits
invites the reader to assume the maximum.

## §39. The transcript standard

A transcript is not a list of grades; it is a document a stranger must be able to
evaluate. Every Al-Madeenah transcript carries:

Institution block (legal name, registration standing as it truly is, contact, verification
URL) · student identity and reference · programme and pathway · **for each course**: code,
Arabic title, English title, level, NLH, grade band, attempts, date · cumulative NLH ·
**the grade scale printed on the document itself** · progression status · completion
status · issue date · credential ID · verification URL.

**The grade scale is printed on every transcript.** A registrar in Riyadh or Toronto
cannot look up a scale they have never encountered, and a transcript that assumes they can
is a transcript that will be set aside.

## §40. The register is the credential

**[Binding]** The paper *expresses* the credential. The **public verification register**
*is* the credential.

The register is deliberately separate, deliberately dull, and deliberately permanent. It
takes a credential ID and returns: whether it is valid, what it attests, to whom, when
issued, and whether it has been revoked. Nothing else. It carries no marketing, requires
no account, and is free to everyone forever (`§6.1`). It is the last thing that should
ever be switched off, and it is designed so that it could outlive the rest of the
institution.

## §41. The student record

The student sees simplicity; the institution keeps depth. The record holds: identity,
enrolment, programmes, courses, levels, every attempt, grades, attendance at live
sessions, NLH accrued, assessment artefacts, gate outcomes with named assessors,
progression decisions, credentials issued, payments, scholarships, accommodations,
academic-integrity findings, and every change with who made it and when.

**Amānah (`§7`).** The record is a trust held on the student's behalf. It is not a
marketing asset, it is not sold, it is not mined, and it is retained under `EB` privacy
provisions with one carve-out: **the credential register entry is permanent**, because a
credential that can be deleted cannot be verified.

## §43. Attribution

No scholarly position is stated without a named source. No opinion is attributed to a
school without naming the work. Where scholars differ, the difference is presented as a
difference, with representative positions attributed, and **the College does not
adjudicate** (`§8.3`).

## §44. AI governance

The College may offer a study assistant. **[P]** Its constitution is fixed here in advance,
before it exists, because governance written after a system ships is not governance.

### §44.1 Permitted

Explaining grammar and morphology · generating practice exercises and drills · vocabulary
work · conversational practice · study planning and pacing · explaining a lesson already
authored by a teacher · summarising the student's own progress · answering questions about
how the College works.

### §44.2 Forbidden — absolutely

**[Binding]**

1. **Generating, completing, correcting or reciting Qur'anic text.** It may retrieve and
   display verified text. It may not produce a single word of it.
2. **Generating ḥadīth**, or supplying a chain, grading or attribution not present in the
   retrieved source.
3. **Issuing fatwā**, or answering any question of the form *"is it permissible…"*, *"must
   I…"*, *"is my ʿibādah valid…"*. It states that the College does not issue fatwā, and
   refers on.
4. **Presenting itself as a scholar, muftī, shaykh, muqriʾ or teacher.** It is a study
   assistant and says so when asked.
5. **Assessing, grading, or contributing to a mastery gate.** `§30`.
6. **Inventing a citation, a chain, a scholar, or a work.**

### §44.3 Behaviour

The assistant is **quiet**. It does not greet, does not volunteer, does not interrupt a
lesson, and does not ask how it can help. It waits beside the text under a plain label —
*Ask about this lesson* — and it knows the lesson it is beside.

Every reply that touches sacred text or scholarly opinion carries its source. A reply the
assistant cannot source is a reply it does not give; **"I don't know, and here is who to
ask"** is a correct and complete answer.

### §44.4 Accountability

Every assistant interaction is logged with its retrieved sources and is auditable by the
Academic Board. A student may always reach a human, and the route to one is never more
than one action away from the assistant.

## §47. How the College says difficult things

Three situations recur, and each has a fixed form:

**When we do not have something yet.** Name the absence, say what it will take, do not

> *"This attempt did not meet the criterion for sustained accuracy in the fifth juzʾ.
> Here is the specific pattern of error, and here is the plan. Retakes are free and
> unlimited."*

The public website carries the strongest ceremonial expression. **The Study is quieter by
design** — ivory and white working surfaces, royal blue for navigation and hierarchy,
gold reduced to indicators. A student is there to concentrate for an hour, not to be
impressed for ten seconds. Applying the homepage's ceremony to a lesson screen would be a
failure of understanding, not a triumph of consistency.

## §58. What "royal" means, and what it does not

| Royal is | Royal is not |
|---|---|
| Composed, rare, immaculate, deliberate, calm, warm, timeless | Crowns, palaces, glitter, faux-gold, black-and-gold, ornament everywhere |
| Confidence without volume | Decoration as a substitute for quality |
| Precision, proportion, typography, whitespace, restraint, consistency | Animation as a substitute for hierarchy |

**The luxury formula:** exceptional typography + exceptional spacing + exceptional
photography + exceptional motion + exceptional writing + exceptional consistency —
**instead of** decoration, not in addition to it.

**The final colour test.** Remove the wordmark. If colour, typography and composition alone
still say *Al-Madeenah*, the identity is working.

# PART XIII — DESIGN EDITORIAL PRINCIPLES

## §59. What governs what

`DX` governs execution. This Part governs the *editorial* decisions design must obey.

1. **Photography never invents institutional fact.** **[Binding]** No generated or stock
   image may depict a person as Al-Madeenah faculty, staff or student until real
   photographs of real people exist with consent. Where we have none, we use manuscript,
   text, geometry and architecture — never a stock photograph of somebody else's students.
   `EB §46`.
2. **The Qur'an is never an image asset.** `§42`.
3. **Motion has a job or it is removed.** `§60`.
4. **The certificate is a document, not a poster.**
5. **Arabic typography is designed, not substituted.** `§48`.

## §60. The motion language

Six categories. Every animation belongs to exactly one, or it does not ship.

| Category | Job | Duration | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ceremonial** | Major institutional moments — arrival, award | 700–1,200 ms | Slow, weighted, once |
| **Editorial** | Content entering the page | 400–700 ms | Gentle, staggered, once per element |
| **Academic** | Progress, mastery, gates | 400–900 ms | Purposeful; conveys state change |
| **Navigation** | Menus, panels, transitions | 250–420 ms | Immediate, confident |
| **Micro** | Buttons, controls, focus, press | 100–200 ms | Instant. Press must never feel deferred. |
| **Ambient** | Subtle depth | Continuous only where `EB` permits | Barely perceptible |

**[Binding]** Every category has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback, enforced in the test
suite. **Press feedback survives reduced motion** — that setting asks for no animation, not
for no feedback (`DX §28`).

**[Binding] No infinite animation** except an indeterminate busy indicator carrying
`aria-busy`, which must also be cancelled under reduced motion. Enforced in the build.

**A single beautifully executed transition is worth more than ten simultaneous ones.**

## §61. SHRS → Al-Madeenah design transfer matrix

**Source and its limits, stated plainly.** The live sites `shroyalschools.ng` and
`shroyalschools.com` were **not reachable** from this environment — the network gateway
refused the connection, as it now does for our own deployment domain. **I have not seen
the live site and do not claim to have.** This matrix is derived from the SHRS
**repository** study recorded in `docs/11-shrs-feature-register.md`: 148 pages, ~10,000
lines of CSS across 13 layers, 74 client modules, 40+ Cloudflare Functions over D1, and
109 institutional documents. **[V] — should be re-run against the live site when a
network path exists.**

**This is principle extraction, not asset transfer.** No SHRS graphic, photograph, layout,
component, wording or code is reused.

| SHRS element | What it achieves | Ruling | Al-Madeenah replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense mega-menu over many sections | Signals institutional breadth | **Adapt** | Three-panel register menu. Breadth we do not have may not be simulated (`§10`) |
| Sticky header, state change on scroll | Persistent orientation | **Keep** | Kept; `overflow-x: clip` so it cannot break sticky |
| Photographic hero, campus imagery | Immediate proof of a real place | **Reject** | We have no campus and no photographs. Typographic cover: constructed geometry, both scripts, no invented place |
| Statistic counters (students, years, staff) | Instant credibility | **Reject** | Every number would be invented. Replaced by *falsifiable* claims — published gates, published hours |
| Auto-rotating carousels | Fits much into little | **Reject** | Motion without a job (`§60`); an accessibility and comprehension cost |
| Marquee / ticker strips | Energy | **Reject** | `§60`. Dated, and unreadable on a 320px phone |
| News and events feed | Aliveness | **Adapt** | Institutional announcements, dated, attributed, never deleted (`§53`) |
| Card grid for programmes | Scannability | **Adapt** | Four schools as an editorial quad; content-led, not card-led |
| Heavy multi-column footer | Completeness | **Adapt** | The seal footer: crest, bays, warrants, plinth — a conclusion, not a link dump |
| 13 CSS layers, 74 client modules | Feature depth | **Reject** | Static generation, no runtime framework. `EB §24` |
| D1-backed portal and functions | Real operations | **Adapt later** | The Study, when there are students. **[P]** |
| Distinct African institutional confidence | Presence without apology | **Keep — the most valuable thing in the study** | Carried into type, scale and colour weight, expressed in an editorial rather than promotional register |

**The relationship to aim for:** two institutions that visibly share an intelligent design
heritage, and that nobody would mistake for the same website with a different logo.

## §62. Every claim is classified before publication

**[Binding] An aspiration may never be published in the grammar of a fact.**

### §62.1 Never invented

**[Binding]** Student numbers · faculty credentials · accreditation · partnerships ·
campuses · alumni · awards · rankings · founding dates · years of operation · testimonials
· photographs of people presented as our people · endorsements.

This is enforced in the build, not only asserted here: the static suite fails on
unevidenced superlatives, and it has caught **two** of my own violations in this

## §63. Accreditation

**[V]** The College holds no accreditation and claims none. The word does not appear on any
surface except to say so.

**The roadmap, honestly stated:**

| Question | Standing |
|---|---|
| What may the College offer today? | Instruction, assessment, and its own certificates, clearly described as its own **[R]** |
| What requires registration? | Operating as an educational institution in Nigeria; and possibly other markets **[V]** |
| What requires accreditation? | Any award using a regulated term (`§37.1`); any school-stage certificate (`§23`) |
| Which body governs Arabic and Islamic studies school stages in Nigeria? | **NBAIS** — National Board for Arabic and Islamic Studies **[F]**; its current requirements **[V]** |
| What evidence must be collected from day one? | Everything in `§41`. A record that begins the day accreditation is sought is a record that cannot support it. |

**The design consequence is the point:** the student record, the transcript standard, the
NLH accounting and the credential register are all built now, to the standard an external
evaluator would require, *before* anyone asks for them. That is the cheapest possible time
to build them, and the only time it is possible to do honestly.

# PART XVI — THE "LESS, BUT BETTER" RULE

## §66. Three doors, and one register

**[Binding]** Maximum three authenticated experiences, plus one deliberately independent
public register:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The public website** | Everyone, before signing in |
| 2 | **the Study** — الدراسة | One role-based environment: student, teacher, examiner, registrar, advisor, admissions, finance, support |
| 3 | **The Executive Console** | Founder, board, leadership |
| — | **The Credential Verification Register** | Public, no account, free forever, deliberately separate and deliberately dull (`§40`) |

**No parent portal. No alumni portal. No partner portal. No donor portal. No admissions
portal. No separate LMS.** Role-based permission inside one system, always. A parent is a
person holding a relationship to the College, not a different product.

## §67. The simplicity test

Every proposed feature, page, programme and system answers all eight. A weak answer to any
one is a removal.

1. Does the student need it?
2. Does the teacher need it?
3. Does the institution need it?
4. Can it be merged with something that already exists?
5. Can automation replace it?
6. What complexity does it create?
7. What maintenance does it create in ten years?
8. Does it improve educational outcomes?

## §68. The prestige-and-access paradox, solved explicitly

**Prestige comes from quality, never from exclusion.**

| Money buys | Money never buys |
|---|---|
| More live contact time | A grade |
| Smaller sections | A gate |
| Priority support | A level |
| Additional tutoring | A credential |
| Expedited administration | A place |
| Richer feedback | Standing |

**[Binding]** Service tier is **never** displayed to a teacher at the point of assessment,
and **never** appears on a certificate, a transcript, or the register. A tier a teacher can
see is a tier that will eventually influence a mark.

**Nothing in the interface, the copy or the credential distinguishes a sponsored student
from a paying one.** A student on full scholarship and a student paying the highest tier
hold identical documents and identical standing.

## §69. The final test

> A student from Lagos, London, Riyadh, Dakar and Paris each open Al-Madeenah for the
> first time. Can each of them understand what we are, trust us, afford an appropriate
> pathway, enrol easily, study successfully, receive a credible academic record, and
> continue — without the system becoming complicated?

Any *no* is an instruction. This test is run as a written walkthrough at every major
revision and recorded (`docs/10-nine-persona-walkthrough.md` is its first instance).

**We do not stop at "complete." We stop when the institution is coherent.**

# PART XVII — PROVISIONS ADDED BY THE ADVERSARIAL AUDIT

These eight provisions did not exist in the draft. They were written in response to
`docs/13-bible-adversarial-audit.md`, which found three failures and five gaps. They are
kept in a separate Part rather than folded silently into the text above, because the
record of *what the audit caught* is worth more than a document that looks as though it
was right the first time.

## §70. Safeguarding — `AUD-1`

**[Binding] The College will not enrol any person under 18 until every provision of this
section is operative and a named Safeguarding Lead is appointed.** `§1` lists children and
teenagers among those the College may serve and `§23` describes stages ordinarily studied
by minors; until this section is live, both are read as excluding minors. The College may
not serve children before it can protect them, whatever that costs in enrolment.

Distance makes this harder, not easier. There is no corridor, no open door, and no
colleague walking past. The controls must therefore be explicit.

### §70.1 When operative

| Rule | |
|---|---|
| **No adult is ever alone with a minor in a private session** | **[Binding]** Minors are taught in groups, or with a parent/guardian present, or with a second member of staff present |
| **Every session involving a minor is recorded and retained** | Retained for a defined period; accessible to the parent/guardian on request |
| **No private channel** | No staff–minor contact by personal phone, personal email, or any messaging outside the College system. **[Binding]** |
| **Vetting** | Anyone teaching or assessing a minor is identity-verified, reference-checked, and cleared to the standard of their jurisdiction, recorded before first contact **[V]** |
| **Parental consent** | Recorded at enrolment; the guardian has standing access to the record, the recordings and the teacher |
| **Reporting route** | A named Safeguarding Lead, reachable directly by a student or guardian, **structurally independent of the teaching line** — a concern about a teacher never routes through that teacher's colleague |
| **Data minimisation** | Minors' data is collected to the minimum, never used for marketing, never used for analytics **[Binding]** |
| **Training** | No one teaches a minor without recorded safeguarding training |

### §70.2 Adult students

Safeguarding does not end at 18. A vulnerable adult, a student in distress, and a student
disclosing harm are all foreseeable in an institution of any size. The same reporting
route serves them, and the College's duty is to **refer competently, not to counsel** —
staff are teachers, not clinicians, and pretending otherwise causes harm.

## §71. Arrangements between men and women — `AUD-2`

For a large part of the intended student body this determines whether enrolment is
possible at all. It is therefore stated in advance, on the public admissions page, rather
than left for a student to discover after paying.

**[I]** The College's arrangements:

1. **Women's programmes are taught by women wherever a qualified teacher is available**,
   and expanding that availability is an appointment priority rather than an aspiration.
2. **No one-to-one private live session between a teacher and a student of the opposite
   gender is scheduled by default.** Where one is academically necessary, the student
   chooses among: a same-gender teacher, a third party present, or a recorded session.
   **The choice belongs to the student and costs nothing.**
3. **Group live sessions** are offered in segregated sections where demand supports it,
   and the section type is stated before enrolment.
4. **Camera is never required.** **[Binding]** Full participation, full assessment and
   every gate are reachable by audio alone. A student who does not appear on video is not
   disadvantaged in any assessment, and identity confirmation at gates is designed
   accordingly (`§33.2`). This is also the correct engineering decision (`§27.2`) — the
   two requirements happen to agree.
5. **Adab in every session** is the standard for everyone, and is part of the teaching
   rather than a rule imposed on students.

The College states its own arrangements and does not characterise anyone else's.

## §72. Assessor independence — `AUD-3`

The whole academic model rests on an assessor's willingness to write *lam yablugh* on the
attempt of a student paying the highest tier. Culture will not hold that. Structure must.

**[Binding]**

1. **At the point of assessment the assessor cannot see** the student's service tier,
   scholarship status, payment status, or fee band. Not "should not" — **cannot**: the
   assessment interface does not carry the fields.
2. **Assessment outcomes are never used as a performance measure for the assessor.**
   Teacher review draws on observation, moderation agreement, student feedback and
   professional development — never on pass rates. `§28` QA cycle.
3. **No pass-rate target may exist anywhere in the institution**, in any plan, forecast,
   budget or dashboard. A forecast that assumes a completion rate is a forecast that has
   created an incentive.
4. **No commercial role has authority over an academic outcome.** A gate outcome may be
   changed only through moderation (`§34`) or appeal (`§34`), both of which are academic
   processes. Not by finance, not by admissions, not by the Founder.
5. **Protected disclosure.** Any member of staff may report pressure on an academic
   outcome directly to the Academic Board, and the College may take no adverse action for
   a report made in good faith.

**The Executive Console shows completion data and revenue data. It does not permit anyone
to act on the first in order to move the second.** `§24` of the Mandate is read subject to
this section.

## §73. Language of instruction — `AUD-4`

**[R]** A student must be able to tell, before enrolling, what language they will be
spoken to in.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The language of the College** | **الفصحى** — Modern Standard and classical Arabic. This is what is taught and what is assessed. |
| **Levels 100–200** | Bilingual delivery. Arabic is the object and the medium increasingly; a **support language** carries explanation. The support language of each section is declared before enrolment. |
| **Levels 300–400** | **Arabic-only delivery.** Stated at the outset so it is a goal, not an ambush. |
| **Support languages** | English and Arabic at launch. **French [P]** — a stated priority, because Francophone West Africa is named in the College's own purpose and serving it in English only would be a contradiction. |
| **Colloquial varieties** | **Not taught.** A student wanting Levantine, Egyptian or Gulf dialect is told plainly that this College does not teach it and why: our purpose is access to the tradition, which is written in فصحى. |

Naming what we do not teach is part of `§62`.

## §74. Attendance, engagement, and what the transcript may print — `AUD-5`

`§39` prints a record that a stranger must be able to evaluate, so its terms must mean
something precise.

| Term | Definition | On the transcript? |
|---|---|---|
| **Live-session attendance** | Joined a scheduled live session and participated | **Yes** — as *sessions attended / sessions scheduled* |
| **Notional learning hours** | `§19.1`. Accrued against *submitted evidence of work*, not against elapsed time | **Yes** |
| **Engagement** | Internal signal for student support only | **Never** |

**[Binding]** Logins, time-on-page, video-watched percentage and clicks are **not
attendance, not learning, and never appear on any credential or transcript.** They are
proxies, and a proxy printed on an academic record is a lie with a number attached.

Their only legitimate use is **support**: a student who has stopped is a student to
contact, not a student to penalise (`§22`).

## §75. External examination — `AUD-6`

`§63` claims the College is building to a standard an external evaluator would require. No
framework accepts self-moderation as sufficient, so internal moderation alone does not
meet the claim.

**[P]** Each school appoints an **External Examiner**: a qualified academic with no other
relationship to the College, who annually reviews a sample of gate assessments, the
moderation record, and a sample of credentials against the criteria published for them,
and reports **in writing to the Academic Board**. Reports are retained permanently and are
part of the evidence base for `§63`.

**[Binding] Until an External Examiner is appointed, moderation is internal only, and
every surface describing the College's assessment says exactly that.** An unstated
limitation is a misrepresentation.

## §76. Reasonable adjustments — `AUD-7`

**[Binding]** A student may request an adjustment at any time. The request is decided by
the Registry with an academic, **never by the student's own teacher**, and never requires
the student to explain themselves to the person who will assess them.

**The governing distinction:** an adjustment changes **how a student reaches the
standard**. It never changes **the standard**. Extra time, an audio-only route, an
alternative format, a rescheduled session, a longer maximum registration period, a
different assessment instrument measuring the same competency — all legitimate. A lowered
criterion is not an adjustment; it is a different qualification, and it would be dishonest
to award the same certificate for it.

**[Binding] No adjustment is recorded on any certificate, transcript, or register entry.**
The credential states what was demonstrated. How the student was enabled to demonstrate it
is their business and nobody else's.

## §77. Continuity of the register — `AUD-8`

`§6` requires an institution that outlasts its founder. `§40` requires a credential
register that could outlive the rest of the College. Both were aspirations with no
mechanism. A promise of permanence with no mechanism is a promise that will be broken by
the first hosting invoice nobody pays.

**[Binding]**

1. **Open format.** The register of record is **plain files in a documented, text-based
   format** — not a proprietary database, not a vendor's export. It must be readable by
   someone in thirty years with no access to our software and no knowledge of us.
2. **Reconstructable from one archive.** The entire register must be rebuildable from a
   single archive file, and that property is **tested, not assumed** — an automated
   restoration test that fails the build if it fails.
3. **Three copies, two custodians.** Live, an independent mirror, and an offline archival
   copy held by a second party. **[V]**
4. **Succession clause.** The instrument establishing the College names a custodian who
   receives the register if the College ceases, with the duty to keep verification
   answering. **[V] Legal.** `D-21`.
5. **Verification outlives everything else.** If the College must shut down, the order of
   shutdown is fixed: marketing, then the website, then the Study, and **the register
   last**. A graduate's credential must not stop being verifiable because the institution
   that issued it stopped existing.

# CLOSING DECLARATION

Al-Madeenah International College for Arabic & Islamic Studies is a fully distance-learning
institution. It has, at the date of this document, **admitted no students, appointed no
faculty, issued no credential, and holds no accreditation.** Every one of those facts is
published rather than managed.

What it has is a standard, written down before there was anyone to apply it to, so that it
cannot later be adjusted to fit whoever arrives.

**Fixed standards. Flexible pace. Mastery-based progression. Differentiated service, never
differentiated dignity.**

The College will teach Arabic as the instrument that opens the tradition; the Qur'an as
something retained rather than reached; and the Islamic sciences from named texts with
honest attribution. It will certify only what it can evidence, it will never charge a
student to prove their own education, and it will say plainly what it does not have.

*Wa mā tawfīqī illā bi-llāh.*
