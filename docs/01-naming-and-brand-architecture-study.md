## §3. The finding that reframed the exercise

**The evocative-Arabic-word namespace in online Qur'an and Arabic education is
saturated.** Not partially — comprehensively. Every candidate I tested returned multiple
incumbents *in our exact category*:

| Term | Incumbents found |
|---|---|
| **Itqān** | ITQAN Academy, Itqan ElQuran Academy, Al-Itqan Institute for Islamic & Arabic Studies, Itqan Academy for Quran Learning, Itqan Network — **five, all online Qur'an/Arabic academies** |
| **Suffa / Suffah** | As-Suffa (UK), Suffa Islamic Seminary (Texas), As-Suffah Academy (UK), Al-Suffah Academy (Pakistan), Al-Suffa Islamic Academy, **DHA Suffa University** (a chartered university) |
| **Riwāq** | Riwaq Al-Quran, Riwaq Al-Azhar, Al Riwaq Institute, Riwaq Institute, Riwaq (Palestine) |
| **Sankoré** | **SANKORE'® Institute of Islamic-African Studies International** — a *claimed registered mark* |
| **Bayān / Bayyinah** | Bayyinah Institute, Al-Balagh Academy, multiple |
| **Qalam** | Qalam Institute (US), Al-Qalam Institute (UK) |
| **Madinah** | Islamic University of Madinah, Taibah University, **Al-Madinah International University (MEDIU)**, Madinah Quran Institute |

**Three consequences follow, and they drive everything below:**

1. **Distinctiveness cannot come from the word.** It must come from *construction* — a
   composite name that is registrable as a whole even where its parts are not.
2. **A "beautiful Arabic word" strategy is the single most crowded strategy available.**
   It feels distinctive and is the opposite.
3. **The unoccupied terms are unoccupied for a reason** — they are hard to pronounce, hard
   to spell, or both. Scarcity and usability are inversely correlated across this entire
   namespace. That tension is the real design problem, and no name resolves it perfectly.

## §7. The propriety gate — and what it eliminated

**The matrix was incomplete, and the sensitivity run exposed it.** Scoring "Islamic
authenticity" 0–5 lets a name trade a propriety problem against a strong SEO score. Some
problems are not tradeable. A gate is therefore applied **before** the ranking, and it
vetoes regardless of score.

**A candidate is vetoed if it:**

- **V1 — claims something the institution cannot currently back** (`EB §46`)
- **V2 — constitutes self-praise** (*tazkiyah*), or applies a Qur'anic honorific to
  ourselves (`EB §10.2` Rule 2)
- **V3 — signals a sectarian or methodological position** we have committed to teach
  rather than adjudicate (`EB §5.7`, D-05 open)
- **V4 — collides with a live registered or claimed mark** in education services
- **V5 — carries an adverse media or geopolitical adjacency**

| Candidate | Score | Veto | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dār al-Qurrāʾ** ("House of the Reciters") | 4.00, most robust | **V1** | We have **no muqriʾīn on staff and issue no ijāzah** (D-04, `EB §34.7`). An institution named *House of the Reciters* that cannot certify a reciter invites the one question we least want to keep answering. **The matrix winner, vetoed on our own honesty rule.** |
| **Al-Rāsikhūn** ("those firmly grounded in knowledge", Q 3:7) | 4.00 | **V2** | A Qur'anic honorific for the highest class of scholars, applied by a brand-new institution to itself. Also rests on a contested *waqf* reading of 3:7 — a needless exegetical argument to build a masthead on. |
| **Al-Rusūkh** ("depth, firm-rootedness") | 4.00 | *survives* | Names an aspiration, not a claimed status — materially safer than Al-Rāsikhūn. Eliminated on pronounceability instead (rank 8 CONSUMER, 8 AFRICA-1ST). |
| **Sankoré** | 3.85 | **V4** | **SANKORE'®** — a claimed registered mark held by SIIASI in Islamic-African studies. Direct class conflict. Painful: it was the strongest African-positioning candidate in the study. |
| **As-Suffah** | 3.69 | **V3 + V4** | Six incumbents including a chartered university (DHA Suffa). Separately: *ṣuffah/ṣūfī* adjacency reads as a methodological signal in exactly the West African market where D-05 is unresolved. |
| **Dār as-Sunnah** | 3.34 | **V3** | Methodologically marked. Would read as a positional declaration before we have made one. |
| **Ṭaybah / Ṭābah** | 3.87 / 3.81 | **V4** | Taibah University (a Saudi public university *in Madinah*) and Tabah Foundation (Abu Dhabi). Same collision as MEDIU, with less recognition to show for it. |
| **Al-Riwāq al-Burnawiyya** | 3.69 | **V5** | Historically magnificent — the Borno endowment at Al-Azhar, 1258, funding West African students. But an international Islamic institution named for Borno inherits an adverse search adjacency in Western media that no amount of scholarship outruns. Rejected with real reluctance. |
| **Al-Itqān, Al-Bayān, Al-Manārah** | 3.38 / 3.15 / 3.21 | **V4** | Saturated (§3). |
| **Qubāʾ** | 3.67 | — | No veto; eliminated on pronounceability. Reads as "Cuba" on first sight in English and French. |

**After the gate, the highest-ranked surviving candidate is A2 — the adopted name.**

**This is the study's actual result, and it is worth stating plainly:** the Founder's name
survives not because it is strong, but because every distinctive alternative fails on
trademark, saturation, propriety, or pronounceability. That is a weak-form win, and §9 and
§10 exist precisely because it is.

**New standing rule.** The veto gate above is not specific to this exercise. It is added
to the Editorial Bible as a permanent naming rule (`EB §8.4`), binding on every future
programme, school, campus, and sub-brand name.

## §8. Shortlist deep-dive

### 8.1 ADOPTED — Al-Madinah International College of Arabic and Qur'anic Studies

**Strengths.** Maximum first-sight pronounceability across all four target languages.
Immediate semantic recognition — every Muslim on earth knows what Madinah signifies, which
is recognition no coined name buys at any price. Impeccable authenticity. Reads correctly
on an accreditation application and on a certificate. Preserves the Founder's equity and
the existing repository, domain, and conceptual work. Ranks **2nd under the home-market
weighting** — the weighting that decides year one.

**Weaknesses, stated without softening.** Distinctiveness scores 3/5 and SEO 2.5/5. The
head term is unwinnable: we will not outrank the Islamic University of Madinah, Taibah
University, MEDIU, or the city itself, ever. Trademark protection rests on the composite,
not the name — "Al-Madinah" alone is a geographic term and is weakly protectable at best.

**Why the qualifier is not cosmetic.** "…of Arabic and Qur'anic Studies" does four
distinct jobs: it creates a registrable composite where the bare term is not; it produces
a long-tail search string we *can* own; it separates us from MEDIU in the one context that
matters legally; and it states our discipline on every credential we ever issue.
Empirically it is worth **+0.52** in the matrix — the difference between 30th and 4th.

**The borrowed-authority objection, answered.** `EB §31` bans borrowed authority as a
trust signal. Does naming ourselves for a city we have no connection to breach our own
rule? **No, and the distinction matters.** Borrowed authority is *implying an affiliation
or endorsement that does not exist* — Ḥaramayn imagery suggesting sponsorship, a scholar's
photograph without their endorsement. A place-name is an orientation, universally read as
aspiration rather than affiliation, and used that way by MEDIU (Malaysia) and by hundreds
of institutions. **The rule bites only if we ever imply institutional connection to
Madinah, Saudi Arabia.** That implication is now explicitly forbidden — added to the Bible
as `EB §8.5`, with a required disclosure line on the About page.

## §9. Trademark strategy

Filing strategy follows directly from §8.1's weakness: **the composite is the asset; the
bare name is not.**

1. **File the full composite** — *Al-Madinah International College of Arabic and Qur'anic
   Studies* — as a word mark, plus the Arabic wordmark, plus the figurative mark once
   designed. Composites containing descriptive and geographic elements are registrable
   where the elements alone are not.
2. **Do not attempt to register "Al-Madinah" alone.** It will likely fail as geographic,
   and attempting it invites opposition from better-resourced incumbents.
3. **Filing order:** Nigeria (home, first-to-file discipline) → United Kingdom → EU →
   Saudi Arabia / UAE / Qatar → United States → Malaysia. Malaysia last and deliberately:
   it is MEDIU's home market and the highest-friction filing. Nice/Madrid classes 41
   (education) and 16 (printed matter, certificates); consider 9 (downloadable
   educational software) once the LMS ships.
4. **Clearance search before any filing** — including a search against MEDIU's portfolio.
   Counsel required; I have not done and cannot do this.
5. **Defensive registrations:** the Arabic wordmark, and the group name (§12), which is the
   *strongly* protectable asset in the portfolio.
6. **Coexistence, not confrontation.** If MEDIU raises an objection, the target outcome is
   a coexistence agreement delimiting territory and discipline — not litigation, which we
   would lose on resources regardless of merits.

## §10. SEO strategy — concede the head term, own the long tail

**Do not contest "Madinah."** Any budget spent trying is burnt. The strategy is to be
unbeatable on the searches that actually convert.

| Layer | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Brand** | Own the exact composite string and `almadinah.college`. Schema.org `EducationalOrganization` markup, `sameAs` across every owned profile, consistent NAP. |
| **Long tail** | *"Arabic and Qur'an college Nigeria"*, *"mastery-based hifz programme online"*, *"Arabic diploma West Africa"*, *"learn classical Arabic Nigeria"*, *"online Quran memorisation Nigeria"*, French equivalents for Bands D–E (`EB §38.3`). Winnable within 24 months. |
| **Content moat** | `EB §12.2` already mandates publishing curricula, assessment criteria, faculty credentials, and pricing methodology openly. **That corpus is the SEO asset** — nobody else in this category publishes it, so nobody else can rank for it. Our honesty policy and our search strategy are the same policy. |
| **Disambiguation** | A permanent, indexed page: *"Al-Madinah International College and Al-Madinah International University (Malaysia) are unrelated institutions."* Captures misdirected traffic honestly and pre-empts confusion in exactly the place a regulator or partner would look. |
| **Never** | Bidding on MEDIU's brand terms; comparison pages naming them; any tactic in `EB §32.3`. |

## §11. Domain strategy — inferred, not verified

**DNS and `whois` are unavailable in this environment. Everything here is reasoning, not a
lookup. Confidence: Low on availability. This is the first operational task on
ratification.**

**Primary recommendation: `almadinah.college`.**

The `.college` gTLD converts the naming weakness into an asset: it makes an otherwise
crowded string unique, states the institution type in the URL, is far more likely to be
available than any `.com`/`.org` variant, and reads as deliberate rather than
second-choice. `.edu` is not available to us (US-accreditation-gated), and `.ac.ng`
requires Nigerian tertiary accreditation we do not have (D-03).

**Acquire in priority order:**

| 2 | `almadinahcollege.org` / `.com` | Redirect; defensive |

Register all for ≥5 years with privacy, registrar lock, and DNSSEC. Consolidate at one

## §12. Parent group naming architecture

### 12.1 The strategic requirement

§8.1 established that "Al-Madinah" is weakly protectable. **The group name must therefore
carry the portfolio's trademark strength.** This is not a secondary decision — it is where
the defensible mark lives.

Requirements: inherently distinctive (not descriptive, not geographic); registrable across
all target territories; credible endorsing a school, a college, and future ventures;
pronounceable in English, French, Hausa, Arabic; not confusable with any member brand.

### 12.2 The decision

> **Sulaimiy Education Group**
> **مجموعة السليمي التعليمية**
> **Confidence: Medium. Provisional pending one fact only** — whether a group entity is
> already registered under another name. If it is, that name prevails and this is
> withdrawn without argument.

**Reasoning.**

1. **Eponymous names are the global norm for education groups and the most protectable
   class of mark.** A surname is inherently distinctive — the opposite of "Al-Madinah's"
   problem. It is registrable essentially everywhere, with near-zero collision risk.
2. **The equity already exists.** *Abī Sulaimiy College* already carries the family name.
   The group name consolidates recognition rather than creating a third one to fund.
3. **It is honest.** The group *is* a family education group. `EB §46` prefers the true
   description to a constructed one.
4. **It solves the portfolio problem in one move.** A strongly protectable parent mark
   compensates for a weakly protectable child mark — standard portfolio practice.
5. **It scales** to future ventures without re-naming.

**Why not a distinctive Arabic coinage for the group?** It would need its own 36-candidate
study, would hit the same saturated namespace (§3), and would add a third name to build
recognition for. The eponym is available, free, protectable, and already partly known.

**Confidence is Medium, not High, for two reasons:** a group entity may already exist under
another name (factual, unavailable to me), and a founder eponym touches a personal
preference. `EB §8.2`'s transliteration standard applies — **Sulaimiy**, one spelling,
everywhere. One line reverses this if you disagree.

## §13. Endorsement strategy and brand hierarchy

### 13.1 The model — light-touch endorsement (closes D-12)

Selected over a co-equal dual lockup and over a silent parent. A co-equal lockup would
make an international brand carry a domestic parent's recognition burden in markets where
it means nothing; a silent parent forfeits the entire benefit of the umbrella ruling.

**Endorsement line:** *A member of Sulaimiy Education Group* · *عضو في مجموعة السليمي
التعليمية*

| Surface | Group presence |
|---|---|
| Website footer | ✅ Endorsement line, one line, `--hibr-soft` |
| Letterhead, formal correspondence | ✅ Endorsement line, footer position |
| Certificates and transcripts | ✅ Endorsement line, below the seal, never adjacent to it |
| Accreditation and regulatory filings | ✅ Full group disclosure — required, and to our advantage |
| Partnership MOUs | ✅ Full group disclosure |
| Annual report | ✅ Full group context |
| Website header / primary nav | ❌ Never |
| Logo lockup | ❌ Never |
| Advertising, social profiles, app icon | ❌ Never |
| Student-facing LMS chrome | ❌ Never |

**Rule of thumb:** the group appears where **credibility and accountability** are being
established. It never appears where **attention** is being sought.

### 13.2 Hierarchy rules

1. **Al-Madinah leads on every student-facing surface.** No exceptions.
2. **One level of endorsement only.** Schools within the college never carry the group
   line — that would be a third tier, and three tiers is a bureaucracy, not a brand.
3. **Sibling institutions are never cross-marketed to students.** A parent enquiring about
   Al-Madinah is not marketed SH Royal Schools. Referral on request, never on impulse.
4. **Shared services are invisible to students.** Finance, IT, and QA may be group
   functions; a student always deals with Al-Madinah.
5. **Reputational firewall.** Each member is separately accountable. A group endorsement
   is not a guarantee of a sibling's standing, and no member's marketing may imply that
   another member's accreditation, licence, or recognition extends to it. This is both an
   `EB §46` requirement and basic regulatory hygiene.

### 13.3 Naming rules for everything downstream

Binding on all future programmes, schools, campuses, and sub-brands:

- Every new name passes the **§7 veto gate** first, then the §2.1 matrix.
- Programmes are named **descriptively**, never invented — a student must know what it is
  from its name (`EB §8.3`).
- No new sub-brand in the first three years without a Board resolution.
- Campuses are named for their **place**: *Al-Madinah International College — [City]*.
- No initialisms in the first five years (`EB §8.1`).
