# PART I — FOUNDATIONS

## §1. What this document is

An identity system, not a logo. The mark is the smallest and least important component in
it — §6 defines a test the system must pass **with the mark removed**.

**Every component here carries a documented reason to exist.** Where a conventional
component could not be justified, it was removed and the removal recorded (§7, §60). This
is a constraint on the system, not a description of it.

**One rule governs all others.** Where a decision is between the fashionable and the
durable, the durable wins — even where the fashionable is better today. This system is
designed to be **refined** for fifty years and **replaced** never.

---

## §2. Benchmark study — why some identities endure

Examined: Oxford, Cambridge, Yale, Harvard, MIT, Al-Azhar, the Bodleian, the British
Museum, the Library of Congress, the Rijksmuseum, the V&A, the Smithsonian, IRCICA.
Not to imitate — to isolate the mechanism.

**Eight findings. Each became a design rule.**

| # | Finding | Rule adopted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The seal outlives the logo because it has a job.** University seals survive centuries because they *authenticate documents* — a legal-documentary function, not a marketing one. Function preserves form; decoration gets redesigned. | The seal is our **origin artefact**, designed first, with a real authentication function (§18–§25). The wordmark is derived *from* it. The logo is derived last. |
| 2 | **Typographic identities outlive symbolic ones.** MIT, Yale, and the Rijksmuseum are recognisable through type before symbol. Type is infrastructure — used ten thousand times a day, and it does not date the way illustration does. | Typography is the **primary** carrier of identity (§26–§30). The mark is secondary. |
| 3 | **Enduring marks were born in one ink.** They came from engraving, letterpress, and embossing. That is precisely why they still work monochrome, at any size, on any substrate. A mark born in a gradient dies with that gradient. | **Monochrome is the master.** The mark is designed in one ink and colour is applied afterward, never the reverse (§32). |
| 4 | **The system carries recognition, not the mark.** Cover the Bodleian's or the Rijksmuseum's logo and you still know where you are — type, colour, structure, and tone do the work. | The **Removal Test** (§6) is a shipping gate, not an aspiration. |
| 5 | **Documented meaning survives leadership change.** An element nobody can explain is removed by the next director. An element with a written, defensible reason survives them. | Every element documented with its justification. Undocumented elements are defects. |
| 6 | **Restraint means fewer things that can date.** Every additional element is an additional hostage to fashion. | Two typefaces per script. Four colours. One geometric primitive. One pattern system. |
| 7 | **They are institutional, not commercial.** They do not need to convert, so they never chase attention. Their confidence *is* the aesthetic. | No conversion-driven design in the identity layer. Calm as a structural property (`EB §7.2`). |
| 8 | **Change is refinement, never rebrand.** Oxford's arms have been redrawn many times and replaced never. Continuity of *concept* with renewal of *craft*. | §57 permits redrawing on a defined cycle; it forbids replacement without a Board resolution recording why the concept failed. |

**The counter-lesson.** Institutions that lost their identities did so by adopting the
prevailing commercial style of their decade — the 1970s corporate abstraction wave, the
2015–2022 geometric-sans "blanding" wave. Both looked contemporary and both aged in under
ten years. **Contemporaneity is the failure mode, not the goal.**

## §3. Brand philosophy — extended for Phase 4

`EB §7` established: **Madinah is a standard, not a decoration.** Phase 4 extends it into
form.

If Madinah is a standard, the identity must *embody measurement* rather than assert
prestige. This distinction drives everything below: our identity is built from a real
system of proportion used by Arabic calligraphers for a thousand years (§8–§10), not from
a set of visual signals meant to look established.

**We do not decorate to look old. We use a real standard, so we are not dated.**

## §4. Visual philosophy

**Five principles.**

1. **Proportion over ornament.** Beauty arises from correct relationships between parts.
   This is both a design position and the actual method of Islamic art and calligraphy.
2. **The line before the plane.** Structure comes from hairline rules and precise
   alignment, not from filled shapes, shadows, or containers.
3. **Script is the subject.** In an institution whose subject is Arabic and the Qur'an,
   letterform is not a styling choice. It is the material.
4. **Silence is a material.** Whitespace, unfilled ground, and the absence of sound are
   used deliberately and count as design decisions.
5. **One ink first.** Everything is designed to survive reduction to a single ink on
   paper. Colour, screens, and motion are applications of that master, never its origin.

## §5. Symbol philosophy

**A symbol earns its place by being true, not by being evocative.**

Three tests, all of which must pass:

| | Test |
|---|---|
| **T1 — Historical** | Can the element be traced to a documented, citable practice in the Islamic scholarly tradition? |
| **T2 — Institutional** | Does it describe *this* institution specifically, rather than Islamic education generally? |
| **T3 — Reductive** | Does it survive reduction to 16px, to one ink, to a blind deboss with no ink at all? |

The crescent-and-star fails T1 (a national flag device, not a religious one). The dome and
the minaret fail T2 (they describe mosques, not colleges). The open book fails T2 and T3.
The graduation cap fails T1 and T2. All are already banned at `EB §7.4` and `EB §18.1`;
this article explains the mechanism behind those bans so future decisions can apply it.

## §6. The logo-free identity kernel, and the Removal Test

> **"The institution's credibility should remain recognisable even when the logo is
> removed."**

This is made operational, not aspirational.

### 6.1 The kernel — six carriers, none of which is the mark

| # | Carrier | Why it identifies us |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | **The type pairing** — Source Serif 4 + IBM Plex Sans / IBM Plex Sans Arabic | A single superfamily spanning both scripts is rare in this category and instantly distinguishing |
| K2 | **The palette** — lapis, illumination gold, turquoise, limestone | Rooted in the pigments of Qur'anic illumination, not the emerald-and-gold default (`EB §7.4`) |
| K3 | **Hairline structure** — rules and ledgers, never cards and shadows | Documentary, not commercial |
| K4 | **The nuqṭah grid and pattern** (§35) | Derived from our own geometry; regenerable at any scale |
| K5 | **Bilingual parity layout** — Arabic set with equal weight, never subordinated | Almost no competitor does this; visible at a glance |
| K6 | **Tone** — specificity, no superlatives, published limitations (`EB §10`) | Recognisable in a single paragraph of copy |

### 6.2 The Removal Test — a shipping gate

Before any surface ships:

1. Remove every instance of the mark, the seal, and the institution's name.
2. Show it to ten people who know the institution.
3. **If fewer than seven identify it, the surface fails** and returns to design.

A surface that depends on its logo is under-designed. Recorded per surface in the QA log
(`EB §45.1`, Gate 4).

## §7. Coat of arms — examined and rejected

The brief permits a coat of arms "only if historically and institutionally justified." It
is not, on four independent grounds. Recording the reasoning matters more than recording
the decision, because the pressure to adopt one will recur.

| | Ground |
|---|---|
| **1. No grant exists.** | European heraldry is a *legal* tradition. Arms are granted by an authority — the College of Arms, the Court of the Lord Lyon, a state herald. A Nigerian institution founded in 2026 holds no grant and no lineage. Assuming arms without a grant is, in the tradition's own terms, not a stylistic liberty but an assumption of standing. |
| **2. It is borrowed authority.** | `EB §31` forbids borrowed authority as a trust signal. Arms exist to signal antiquity and inherited status. Adopting them to look established is precisely the thing our own constitution bans. Fails the naming veto gate **V1** (`EB §8.4`) on the same reasoning that killed *Dār al-Qurrāʾ*. |
| **3. Its vocabulary is not ours.** | Helms, crowns, crosses, supporters, mantling, and motto ribbons are Christian-European chivalric devices. Importing them into an Islamic institution's identity is a category error before it is an aesthetic one. |
| **4. There is an authentic alternative, and it is better.** | Islamic chanceries, scholars, endowments, and institutions authenticated documents with the **khātam** (seal) and the **ṭughrā** — *calligraphic* devices, in one ink, whose entire purpose was authentication. That tradition is genuinely ours, genuinely documentary, and genuinely built for the job we need done. |

**Decision: no coat of arms, ever.** The seal system (§18–§25) is built on the khātam
tradition instead. Confidence: **High**.

# PART II — THE MARK

## §8. The nuqṭah — the institutional atom

**The rhombic dot.** One element carries the entire system, and it does so because of two
independently documented historical facts that converge on exactly our two disciplines.

### 8.1 Fact one — the dot is the unit of all Arabic proportion

**Ibn Muqla (d. 940) — or, on the more careful reading of the sources, Ibn Muqla and his
brothers** — are credited with originating **al-khaṭṭ al-mansūb**, "proportioned script,"
subsequently refined by Ibn al-Bawwāb and Yāqūt al-Mustaʿṣimī. In it, every letterform is
derived from three measurements:

1. the **nuqṭah** — the rhombic dot produced by one pressed stroke of the reed pen;
2. the **height of the alif** — expressed in dots (7 in thuluth, 8 in muḥaqqaq, 6 in tawqīʿ);
3. the **circle** whose diameter equals the height of the alif, which governs every curve.

Refined by Ibn al-Bawwāb and Yāqūt al-Mustaʿṣimī, this system is used by calligraphers to
this day. **It is a real, living, thousand-year-old standard of measurement.**

### 8.2 Fact two — the dot was invented to protect Qur'anic recitation

Abū al-Aswad al-Duʾalī introduced dotting (*iʿjām* / *naqṭ*) after hearing a reader
misrecite a verse in a way that changed its meaning, at the request of the governor of
Basra, precisely so that people would read the Qur'an correctly. The system was later
refined by al-Khalīl ibn Aḥmad al-Farāhīdī.

### 8.3 Why this is our symbol and no one else's

| The dot is… | Which is… |
|---|---|
| the unit of measure for all Arabic letterform | **the Arabic language** — our first discipline |
| the instrument invented to preserve correct recitation | **the Qur'an** — our second discipline |
| a standard of proportion | **`EB §7.1`** — "Madinah is a standard" |
| the smallest mark that distinguishes one letter from another | **`EB §33`** — *itqān*: precision is the whole of the thing |

**Two disciplines, one primitive, both documented.** Passes T1, T2, and T3 (§5). A rhombus
survives 16px, one ink, and an inkless deboss.

### 8.4 Geometric specification

A rhombus formed by a square pressed at the reed-pen angle. **All institutional geometry
derives from it.**

```
        ◆        Angle of the qalam nib:  70°  (system constant — see note)
       ╱ ╲       Rhombus:  a square rotated so its long axis sits at the nib angle
      ╱   ╲      Aspect ratio:  1 : 1  square, presented on the diagonal
       ╲   ╱     Notation:  1 n  (one nuqṭah) = the system's base unit
        ╲ ╱
         ◆       Alif height:  7 n     (thuluth proportion — the classical standard)
                 Governing circle Ø:  7 n
```

**`1 n = 8 px` on screen** — which makes the nuqṭah identical to the Bible's 8px baseline
unit (`EB §17`). The identity grid and the interface grid are the same grid. Not a
coincidence: it was chosen so that the institution's spatial system and its symbolic system
are one system.

**`1 n = 2 mm` in print** at standard scale, scaling proportionally.

**Two declared decisions, not historical assertions.** (a) Nib angle varies by script,
region, and calligrapher; there is no single "classical" value. **70° is adopted as this
institution's constant**, informed by naskh practice, and fixed so the geometry is
reproducible. (b) The 7-dot alif is the *thuluth* proportion, while the wordmark (§12) is
naskh, whose metrics differ. **The 7-dot alif is adopted as the institution's module**
because thuluth is the ceremonial script from which the seal derives; the naskh wordmark is
then **optically fitted** to that module rather than metrically derived from it. Declared
here rather than disguised — a calligrapher would notice immediately.

## §9. The alif — the standard

**ا** — the first letter, a single upright stroke, and the reference against which every
other letter in the script is measured.

Set at its classical thuluth proportion of **7 n**, inscribed in the governing circle of
diameter 7 n. The alif is not stylised, not modernised, not given a serif or a taper beyond
what the qalam produces. **It is drawn as the pen makes it**, because the whole point is
that it is the standard, not our interpretation of one.

## §10. The mark — construction

The mark is **the alif, its governing circle, and the dot that measures it.**

```
    ┌─────────────────────────────┐
    │      ╭───────────╮          │   Governing circle:  Ø 7 n, hairline
    │    ╭─┤           ├─╮        │   Alif:  vertical stroke, 7 n high,
    │    │ │     │     │ │        │         centred on the circle's axis
    │    │ │     │     │ │        │   Nuqṭah:  1 n, on the baseline,
    │    ╰─┤     │     ├─╯        │            offset 1 n right of the alif
    │      ╰─────┼─────╯          │            (i.e. beneath it, in the
    │            │  ◆             │             reading direction)
    │      ─ ─ ─ ┴ ─ ─ ─          │   Baseline:  hairline, extends 1 n each side
    └─────────────────────────────┘
```

**Every element justified:**

| Element | Reason |
|---|---|
| The alif | The standard by which script is measured (§9) |
| The circle | Ibn Muqla's third measurement; governs all curved letters (§8.1) |
| The dot | The unit of proportion and the guardian of recitation (§8.2–8.3) |
| The baseline | The line of writing itself — the only line on which Arabic sits |
| **Nothing else** | Anything further would be decoration (`EB §21.2`) |

**Not a letter that spells anything.** The mark is deliberately *not* a word — not "iqraʾ,"
not a monogram, not the institution's initials. It is the *measuring apparatus* of the
script. This avoids the entire class of problems that comes with rendering a word as a
logo: it cannot be misread, cannot be mistranslated, carries no grammatical case, and does
not compete with the Qur'anic text it will sometimes appear near (`EB §18.3.4`).

**Drawn, not typeset.** Commissioned from a named calligrapher (§58), with full rights
assignment, and then vectorised to the geometry above.

## §11. Responsive logo system

Five registers. **Each is a distinct drawing, not a scaled copy** — stroke weights are
optically corrected per register, as they must be.

| R | Register | Contents | Size range | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | **Nuqṭah** | The dot alone | 16–23 px · 4–7 mm | Favicon, app icon, embroidery, watermark, letterhead reverse |
| **R2** | **Mark** | Alif + circle + dot | 24–89 px · 8–24 mm | Avatar, social profile, small signage, ID card |
| **R3** | **Compact lockup** | Mark + Arabic wordmark | 90–129 px · 25–33 mm | Mobile header, social banner, narrow applications |
| **R4** | **Standard lockup** | Mark + bilingual wordmark | ≥130 px · ≥34 mm | Site header, letterhead, presentations, publications |
| **R5** | **Seal** | Full institutional seal (§19) | ≥40 mm, print only | Certificates, transcripts, legal documents, ceremonial |

**Register selection is by available size, never by preference.** A designer who uses R2
where R4 fits has made an error, not a choice.

**R1 is the endurance case.** At 16px the dot is four pixels of solid ink and is still
unmistakably ours, because the *pattern system* (§35) is built from the same shape. This is
the concrete answer to why the primitive is a rhombus and not something more elaborate.

## §12. Arabic wordmark

**Primary. Drawn first. The English wordmark is derived from it, not the reverse.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Full form** | كلية المدينة الدولية للغة العربية وعلوم القرآن |
| **Short form** | كلية المدينة |
| **Script** | Naskh, drawn — a commissioned refinement, not typeset Amiri |
| **Why naskh** | The script of scholarship, of the muṣḥaf, and of reading. Thuluth is ceremonial and is reserved for the seal (§20). Ruqʿah is informal. Kufic is archaising and would be a costume. |
| **Vocalisation** | **None.** Institutional names are not vocalised; tashkīl on a wordmark reads as a teaching aid, not a name. |
| **Construction** | Set to the same 7 n alif height as the mark, so wordmark and mark share one proportional system |

**Binding rules:** never stretched, never letter-spaced (breaks the joins — `EB §15.3.4`),
never faux-bolded, never set in a UI face, never re-typeset by anyone. It is a drawing.

## §13. English wordmark

Drawn from **Source Serif 4 Semibold**, then customised so it is ownable rather than merely
typeset: tightened spacing, redrawn terminals on the *A* and *M*, and a corrected optical
relationship between "AL-MADINAH" and the descriptor line.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Full form** | AL-MADINAH INTERNATIONAL COLLEGE OF ARABIC AND QUR'ANIC STUDIES |
| **Short form** | AL-MADINAH COLLEGE |
| **Case** | Small caps for the institution name; upper-and-lower for the descriptor line |
| **Tracking** | +0.06 em on the small-cap line only |
| **Apostrophe** | Typographic ʾ (U+02BE, modifier letter right half ring) in "Qurʾanic" — not a straight quote, not an apostrophe |

**Why a serif and not a geometric sans.** The 2015–2022 wave of institutions converting to
geometric sans wordmarks is the single clearest dateable marker of that decade (§2,
counter-lesson). A text serif with a real historical lineage was already old and is
therefore not aging. This is a durability decision, not a taste one.

## §14. Bilingual lockups

**Arabic leads.** In every lockup, in both directions.

| Lockup | Arrangement |
|---|---|
| **Stacked** (default) | Mark centred · Arabic wordmark beneath at 1 n gap · hairline rule 1 n below · English wordmark 1 n below the rule |
| **Horizontal — LTR pages** | Mark · 2 n · Arabic wordmark over English wordmark, left-aligned |
| **Horizontal — RTL pages** | Arabic wordmark over English wordmark, right-aligned · 2 n · mark |
| **Seal** | Circular, both scripts in the ring (§19) |

**Parity rules — binding:**

1. The Arabic wordmark's **optical** weight equals or exceeds the English. Never smaller,
   never lighter, never secondary in position.
2. The hairline rule between them is a **separator, not a hierarchy** — equal space above
   and below, always.
3. The mark **never sits between** the two scripts. It sits before both, or above both.
4. **Never a single-script lockup in a bilingual context.** English-only lockups are
   permitted only where Arabic is genuinely unsupported by the substrate, and that is a
   recorded exception, not a designer's call.

## §15. Clear space

**Expressed in the institution's own unit**, which means it scales correctly by
construction and cannot be mis-specified.

| Register | Clear space, all four sides |
|---|---|
| R1 Nuqṭah | **2 n** |
| R2 Mark | **2 n** |
| R3 / R4 Lockups | **3 n** |
| R5 Seal | **1 × the seal's own radius** |

Nothing enters the clear space — no type, no rule, no image edge, no fold, no trim, no
other logo. On a photographic ground, the clear space must also be visually clear: if the
image is busy within it, the mark moves or the image changes.

### 15.1 Non-visual identity

The §6 kernel is entirely visual, which means a blind student has no access to any of it.
Four requirements close that gap:

1. Every SVG carries a `<title>` as its first child.
2. The mark's accessible name is **the institution's name in the page's language** — never
   "logo", never "icon", never the filename.
3. **One alt-text convention across every surface**, recorded in the asset library, so the
   identity is as consistent in a screen reader as it is on screen.
4. **The institution's name always appears as real text adjacent to the mark.** The mark
   never replaces the name. This is also why R1 is never used alone on a page that does not
   name the institution in text.

## §17. Monochrome, reversal, and misuse

### 17.1 Monochrome is the master (§2, finding 3)

The mark is **designed** in single-ink black and every colour version is derived from it.
Required variants, all drawn and stored, none generated at use time:

| Variant | Application |
|---|---|
| Single ink — `--hibr` on light | Default print, documents |
| Single ink — `--waraq` on dark | Reversed, dark grounds |
| Single ink — `--lazaward` on light | Primary brand application |
| Single ink — `--dhahab` on dark | Ceremonial, certificates |
| **Line-only, no fill** | Embossing, engraving, foil die, etching (§22) |
| **Solid silhouette** | Embroidery, single-colour vinyl, stamps |

**There is no full-colour version of the mark, because the mark has no second colour.**
This is the single most important durability decision in Part II.

### 17.2 Misuse — absolutely forbidden

Recolouring outside the palette · stretching or condensing · rotating (the alif is
vertical; a rotated alif is a different letter) · outlining · drop shadows, bevels, glows ·
gradient fills · placing inside a container shape it was not drawn for · animating as a
loading spinner (`EB §22`) · redrawing or re-typesetting · combining with another mark
without a Board-approved co-branding lockup · placing on the same visual unit as Qur'anic
text (`EB §5.1.5`, `EB §18.3.4`) · using R5 (the seal) as a logo on marketing surfaces ·
adding a tagline inside the clear space.

# PART III — SEALS AND AUTHENTICATION

## §18. Seal philosophy

The seal descends from the **khātam** and **ṭughrā** tradition (§7, ground 4) — Islamic
calligraphic devices whose purpose was to authenticate a document, in one ink, at the point
of issue.

**Three principles.**

1. **A seal authenticates. It does not advertise.** The seal never appears on marketing
   material, social media, merchandise, or a website header. Its appearance is itself
   information: this document is official.
2. **A seal is applied, not printed.** Where physically possible it is debossed, embossed,
   or wet-stamped — a physical act by an authorised person, logged.
3. **The seal is calligraphic, not heraldic.** Its content is script and geometry. It
   carries no crown, wreath, supporter, helm, mantling, or motto ribbon.
4. **No Qur'anic text, and no hadith text, in any seal, ever.** A seal is struck, repeated
   mechanically thousands of times, applied by junior staff, and impressed into surfaces
   without regard to orientation. None of that is compatible with `EB §5.1`'s handling
   requirements. This is stricter than `EB §5.1` alone implies, and it is absolute: the seal
   carries the institution's name, its geometry, and its date — nothing else.

## §19. The institutional seal — master

Circular, because the governing circle of al-khaṭṭ al-mansūb is circular (§8.1). The seal
*is* the governing circle at institutional scale — the same geometry as the mark, enlarged.

```
        ╭─────────────────────────────╮
      ╱   كلية المدينة الدولية للغة      ╲       Outer ring:  Arabic full name,
     │      العربية وعلوم القرآن          │                  naskh, running clockwise
     │   ╭───────────────────────╮   │       Inner ring:  English full name, small caps
     │   │                       │   │       Ring rules:  two hairlines, 1 n apart
     │   │          │            │   │       Field:      the mark (alif · circle · dot)
     │   │          │     ◆      │   │       Base:       foundation year, Hijri / Gregorian
     │   │     ─ ─ ─┴─ ─ ─       │   │
     │   ╰───────────────────────╯   │       NO crown · NO wreath · NO supporters
      ╲   AL-MADINAH INTERNATIONAL  ╱        NO ribbon · NO crescent · NO star
        ╰─ COLLEGE · [AH] / [CE] ─╯
```

**Foundation year is left as `[YEAR]` until the legal entity's registration date is known**
(`EB §46.3`, D-01). A seal carrying an invented founding date would be the most
consequential possible breach of `EB §46` — it is the one element of an institution's
identity that is *specifically* a claim about time.

## §20. Ceremonial seal

| | |
|---|---|
| **Differs from the master by** | Thuluth in the outer ring rather than naskh; finer hairlines; a second concentric rule |
| **Why thuluth** | The ceremonial script of the tradition — used for what is meant to be beheld rather than read at speed |

| **Colour** | One ink only — `--dhahab` on `--waraq`, or blind deboss |

## §21. Academic seal

The working seal. Simplified for repeated use at small size and for high-volume documents.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Differs from the master by** | Single ring; short-form name only (كلية المدينة / AL-MADINAH COLLEGE); no foundation year |

## §22. Embossing seal — physical specification

For blind deboss, wet seal, and engraving. **A distinct drawing**: fills are removed, all
elements become strokes, and strokes are thickened to survive a die.

| Specification | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum stroke | **0.35 mm** | Below this a die does not hold on cotton stock |
| Minimum counter (enclosed space) | 0.5 mm | Below this the counter fills and closes |
| Relief depth | 0.4–0.6 mm | Deeper tears cotton fibre; shallower reads as an accident |
| Die material | Brass, machine-engraved | Photopolymer will not hold 0.35 mm over long runs |
| Substrate minimum | 250 gsm, ≥25% cotton | Lighter stock cracks at the deboss edge |
| Hand-seal minimum | 30 mm | Below this hand pressure is uneven |
| Text in die | **None below 6 pt** | The outer ring's name is dropped from the small die rather than rendered illegibly |

**Custody.** Physical seal dies are numbered, held in a locked register, issued to named
holders, and their use is logged per document. A die is destroyed on retirement and the
destruction witnessed and recorded. **This is a security control, not an administrative
one** (§24, §25).

## §23. Digital seal

Two components that must never be confused, and a rule about what the visible one means.

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Visual token** | The academic seal rendered as SVG for on-screen documents. **Carries no authority whatsoever.** Trivially copied. |
| **Cryptographic seal** | An **Ed25519** detached signature over the certificate's canonical record, issued as a **W3C Verifiable Credential**. This is the actual seal. |

**Binding rule.** Every surface showing the visual token must also show the verification
route. **The image never stands alone as evidence** — that is precisely how digital
credential forgery succeeds.

**Key management — the root of trust for every credential we ever issue.**

| Requirement | Specification |
|---|---|
| Authoritative key source | HTTPS at a stable, documented URL. **DNS TXT is a convenience mirror only** |
| **DNSSEC** | **Mandatory** on the primary domain. Without it a TXT record is trivially spoofable by an attacker with network position — and this key is the root of trust |
| **Key history** | A **signed, append-only** list of every key with its validity window, each new key signing the previous one's retirement |
| Verification rule | A credential is checked against the key **valid at its issue date**, never the current key. Rotation must never silently invalidate years of issued credentials |
| Retired keys | **Never deleted.** Retained permanently |
| Compromised keys | Marked **distinctly** from keys retired on schedule. Credentials under a compromised key are **re-issued**, not trusted |
| Private key custody | Hardware-backed, two-person control, never on a laptop or in a repository |

## §24. Certificate authentication system

**Governing principle, and the one that defeats forgery economically:**

> **The paper is not the credential. The register is the credential.**
> The document is a *receipt* for a register entry. Forging the receipt achieves nothing
> when checking the register is free, instant, and public.

This directly implements `EB §36.1`. It also means our security effort is spent making
**verification trivially easy** rather than making **paper hard to copy** — the latter is an
arms race we would lose; the former is one we simply win.

### 24.1 Six layers

| L | Layer | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Substrate** | 250 gsm, ≥25% cotton rag, UV-dull (unbrightened) stock, true watermark where volume justifies the mould, artificial watermark otherwise |
| **L2** | **Print** | Blind-deboss seal (§22) · guilloche border generated from the nuqṭah geometry (§35.4) · microtext rule at 0.6 pt reading the full institutional name · one UV-reactive ink element, position undisclosed |
| **L3** | **Data** | Certificate number · holder's full name in both scripts · award and level · date in both calendars · named assessor · notional learning hours (`EB §33.2a`) |
| **L4** | **Machine-readable** | QR code resolving to `verify.almadinah.college/<opaque-token>` |
| **L5** | **Cryptographic** | Ed25519 signature over the canonical record; W3C Verifiable Credential; offline-verifiable against the published key |
| **L6** | **Register** | The public verification register. **Source of truth. A certificate with no register entry is void, regardless of how perfect the paper is.** |

**The register is permanent.** A certificate outlives the systems that issued it — a
graduate may present one in forty years. The register is retained indefinitely, never
purged, and included in the institution's succession and teach-out planning as a
Board-level obligation rather than an IT one. An institution that lets its verification
register lapse has retroactively voided every credential it ever issued.

**Permanence applies to the record, not to its publication.** Data-protection law in two of
our named markets grants a right to erasure, and an unqualified "never purged" cannot stand
against it. The resolution — reached in Phase 5 and binding here — is that the **record** is
permanent under a legitimate-interest basis, while **public lookup may be suppressed** at
the holder's request, leaving verification available through the Registry. A **revoked**
credential is never suppressible: suppression is a privacy right, not an escape route. Full
reasoning at `IA §4.1`; requires counsel's confirmation before the first credential is
issued.

### 24.2 Certificate numbering

```
AM-2027-QM-00184-K
│  │    │  │     └── Check character, ISO 7064 MOD 97-10
│  │    │  └──────── Serial within school and year, zero-padded
│  │    └─────────── School code: QM Qur'an Memorisation · TJ Tajwīd
│  │                              AR Arabic · CP Completion
│  └──────────────── Gregorian year of award
└─────────────────── Institution
```

The check character makes a casually invented number fail instantly, before any database
lookup. Serials are **non-sequential within their block** (drawn from a shuffled pool) so
that holding two certificates does not reveal cohort size — a small confidentiality
property that costs nothing to build in and cannot be retrofitted.

### 24.3 The verification page — privacy by design

A public verification endpoint is a public disclosure surface. It is designed as one.

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| The QR encodes an **opaque token** of **≥128 bits of true randomness**, not derived from the certificate number by any function | A QR code is photographed, shared, and posted. It must leak nothing — and token entropy, not rate limiting, is the real control against distributed enumeration |
| The page shows: holder name, award, level, date, status, issuing school | The minimum an employer or registrar needs |
| The page **never** shows: date of birth, address, email, photograph, marks, grades, fees, or any other enrolment record | None of it is needed for verification, and all of it is a disclosure |
| **Status is always shown explicitly** — `Valid` · `Revoked` · `Not found` | `EB §36.6` requires revocation to be public and visible |
| Rate-limited, with no enumeration | Prevents harvesting the register by iterating tokens |
| No login, no tracking, no analytics beyond aggregate counts | A third party verifying a credential is not a marketing lead (`EB §32.3`) |

## §25. Security features against forgery

| Feature | Layer | Defeats |
|---|---|---|
| Public register (L6) | Process | **Everything.** The primary control; all others are secondary. |
| Check-character numbering | Data | Casually invented numbers |
| Cryptographic signature | Digital | Altered PDFs, fabricated digital copies |
| Blind deboss | Physical | Scan-and-reprint (a deboss does not photocopy) |
| Guilloche from our own geometry | Physical | Redrawing; the pattern is generated from a documented, non-public parameter set |
| Microtext | Physical | Photocopying and consumer scanning |
| UV element | Physical | Casual reproduction |
| Cotton, unbrightened stock | Physical | Office-paper reproduction (visibly different under UV) |
| Seal die custody register (§22) | Process | Insider misuse |
| Two-person issuance | Process | Single-actor fraud |

**Explicitly not used:** holograms (consumer-grade ones are easier to fake than to verify,
and they signal "product," not "institution"); gold foil seals (decorative, trivially
sourced); "certified copy" stamps without register backing.

# PART IV — TYPOGRAPHY

## §26. Type philosophy

Confirms `EB §15–§16` and adds the durability reasoning.

**Every typeface in this system is licensed under the SIL Open Font License, and that is a
fifty-year decision, not a budget decision.** A commercial licence can be revoked,
repriced, discontinued, or lost with a foundry. An OFL font cannot be taken away — the
licence is irrevocable and the source is redistributable. For an institution planning in
decades, this is the single most consequential typographic property, and it outranks
aesthetic preference.

**One exception, and it is the system's one real licensing risk:** the muṣḥaf face.

## §27. The type system

| Role | Face | Licence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latin display | **Source Serif 4** | OFL | Wordmark drawn from it (§13) |
| Latin / UI body | **IBM Plex Sans** | OFL | |
| Latin data | **IBM Plex Mono** | OFL | Certificate numbers, verification IDs |
| **Arabic UI / body** | **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** | OFL | Designed alongside its Latin sibling — the reason bilingual harmony is structural here rather than approximated |
| Arabic editorial / display | **Amiri** | OFL | Wordmark drawn from it (§12) |
| Arabic teaching (vocalised) | **Kitab** ⚠ *licence unverified* | ⚠ | Verified fallback: **Amiri** with full tashkīl (OFL). No unverified face ships |
| Arabic ceremonial | **Amiri** (typesetting) + commissioned **thuluth** (seal lettering) | OFL / owned | See §28.6 — Aref Ruqaa was removed from this system |
| **Muṣḥaf** | **KFGQPC Uthmanic Script HAFS** | ⚠ **Proprietary terms** | **D-08.** Fallback: **Amiri Quran** (OFL) |

**D-08 resolution — decided under executive autonomy.** The muṣḥaf face is the only
non-OFL dependency in the entire system. **Amiri Quran is adopted as the specified
fallback and is engineered as a first-class path from day one**, not as a degradation.
KFGQPC is used only if and when its terms are verified in writing as permitting web
embedding, commercial use, and print at our scale. Confidence: **High** on the approach,
because it removes the only single point of licensing failure in the system.

## §28. Arabic typography standards

`EB §15.3`'s ten rules are binding and restated here by reference. Identity-layer additions:

1. **The Arabic is drawn for the wordmark and seal, typeset everywhere else.** Do not
   confuse the two: the wordmark is an object, not a setting.
2. **Naskh for reading, thuluth for beholding, ruqʿah never institutionally.** The scripts
   have registers; using thuluth for body text is as wrong as setting a book in a display
   face.
3. **Never Kufic.** It is archaising. Using a 9th-century angular script to signal
   authenticity is a costume, and costumes date fastest of all.
4. **Tashkīl in identity contexts: never.** Names are not vocalised.
5. **The Arabic sets the leading for both scripts** in bilingual documents — Arabic needs
   more, and reducing it to match Latin is the most common and most damaging bilingual
   typesetting error.
6. **Ruqʿah is not used institutionally, in any face, at any size.** Ruqʿah is historically
   an administrative and rapid-handwriting script — the register of clerks and notes, not of
   ceremony. Aref Ruqaa was specified as this system's "ceremonial" face in an earlier draft
   and has been **removed entirely**: it was available, not suitable, and availability is
   not a reason.
7. **Any element containing tashkīl uses the teaching face, wherever it sits.** Vocalised
   Arabic inside a button, label, table header, or navigation item switches to the teaching
   face at the vocalised size and leading minimums (`EB §15.3.2`) — it does not inherit the
   UI face. IBM Plex Sans Arabic is sans-inflected and handles heavy tashkīl poorly at UI
   sizes. One class, `.text-mushakkal`, carries this, and it is the only correct way to
   render vocalised Arabic anywhere in the system — chrome or content.

## §29. English typography standards

`EB §16.2` binding. Additions:

1. Small caps only where the face provides **true** small caps. Faux small caps are a
   defect.
2. The typographic apostrophe **ʾ** (U+02BE) for the hamza in transliteration — *Qurʾanic*,
   *ijāzah*, *jamīʿ*. Never `'`.
3. Figures: **lining** in tables, certificates, and data; **old-style** in running text.
4. Never letterspace lowercase.

# PART VI — PATTERN, IMAGE, MOTION

## §35. Pattern language

### 35.1 Principle

**The pattern is generated from the institution's own geometry, and it is generated, not
drawn.** A parametric construction from the nuqṭah rhombus and the governing circle —
which means it can be regenerated at any scale, for any substrate, forever, without an
original file to lose. That is a fifty-year property.

**It is explicitly not arabesque wallpaper** (`EB §7.4`), and explicitly not a stock girih
vector.

### 35.2 Construction

The rhombus tiles on a grid of `1 n` at the 70° qalam angle, producing a rhombic lattice.
Three densities:

| Density | Spacing | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Sparse** | 8 n | Large fields, hero grounds, endpapers |
| **Medium** | 4 n | Section transitions, covers, folders |
| **Dense** | 1 n | Guilloche and security borders (§24.1 L2) |

### 35.3 Binding rules

1. **Maximum 6% opacity** behind any text (`EB §21.2.2`).
2. **Never behind Qur'anic text** (`EB §5.1.4`) — no exception, at any opacity.
3. **Never full-bleed at full opacity** on a marketing surface. It is a ground, not a
   subject.
4. **Geometrically correct or it is a defect.** The lattice must tile exactly. This
   institution teaches precision; an approximate pattern is a contradiction.

### 35.4 Security derivative — a separate parameterisation

**The security guilloche is not the ornamental pattern at a smaller size.** An earlier draft
specified it at `1 n` spacing — 2 mm at print scale, which is a decorative border any office
printer reproduces perfectly, providing no security whatsoever.

| Parameter | Ornamental pattern | **Security guilloche** |
|---|---|---|
| Line weight | n/a (fills) | **0.10 mm** |
| Spacing | 8 n / 4 n / 1 n | **0.25–0.40 mm** |
| Generated from | Rhombic lattice | Same geometry, two orders of magnitude finer |
| Parameter set | Published in the asset library | **Non-public**, held with the seal dies under §22 custody |

Requires a press capable of holding 0.10 mm line work — confirmed at §55 before the
specification is frozen.

## §36. Iconography

`EB §19` binding. Identity-layer addition: **the icon grid is the nuqṭah grid** — 24×24 px
= 3n × 3n, 1.5 px stroke, geometric construction, RTL variants for every directional icon.

Custom domain icons — *juzʾ*, *ḥizb*, *sanad*, *ijāzah*, *murājaʿah*, *makhraj*, tajwīd
rules — are drawn specifically for this institution because they are our vocabulary and no
generic set contains them. **These are more distinguishing than the logo** and are part of
the §6 kernel.

## §37. Photography direction

`EB §20` binding, including §20.5's interim rule: **until real, consented photography
exists, no photographs of people ship at all.**

Identity-layer addition — the crop and grade specification, so that when real photography
arrives it is already systematised: available light only; warm, slightly desaturated grade;
shadows held green-black, never blue-black, never crushed to pure black; 35 mm and 85 mm
equivalent focal lengths only; subject off-centre; generous negative space at the
`--jiss` value so type can sit on the image without a scrim.

## §38. Illustration language

`EB §21` binding. Two distinct systems that must never be blended:

| System | Purpose | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **Ornamental** | Structural — transitions, rule terminals, seal surrounds, certificate borders | Geometric, generated (§35). Non-figurative. |
| **Didactic** | Teaching — makhārij diagrams, iʿrāb trees, verb-form tables, tajwīd rule diagrams | Functional, labelled, high-contrast, RTL-aware, accessible. Follows `EB §25`, not `EB §21`. |

**No figurative illustration, no mascots, no character systems, no AI-generated imagery of
people** (`EB §5.3`, `EB §20.3`).

## §39. Motion identity

`EB §22` binding. The identity has **exactly one** permitted animation.

**The alif is written.** A single stroke, drawn in the direction the qalam actually moves —
downward, from the nib's entry. Then the nuqṭah appears. Then the circle, as a hairline.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration | 600 ms total (alif 340 ms · dot 120 ms · circle 140 ms) |
| Easing | `cubic-bezier(.16,.84,.44,1)` — the system's single curve |
| Frequency | **Once per session maximum.** Never on repeat visits, never looping |
| `prefers-reduced-motion` | Renders complete and static. Not shortened — removed |
| Never | As a loading spinner · under a page transition · on certificates · behind Qur'anic text · as a video sting |

**Why this and nothing else.** The mark's meaning is that writing is a measured act. The
only motion that adds meaning is the act of measurement itself. Every other animation would
be decoration, and `EB §21.2.1` removes decoration.

## §48. Mobile application identity

| | |
|---|---|
| **App icon** | R1 nuqṭah, `--waraq` on `--lazaward`, centred, no container decoration, no gradient. Squircle masking handled by the OS — never baked in |
| **Splash** | Static R2 mark on `--lazaward`. **No animation** — a splash animation is seen thousands of times and becomes an irritation, which is the opposite of the §39 rule's intent |
| **Navigation** | RTL-mirrored fully, including gestures and transition direction |
| **Offline state** | A designed state, not an error (`EB §24`) |
| **Notification sound** | **None by default** (`EB §5.5`). Where a sound is required for accessibility, a single unpitched wooden tick — not a musical tone |

## §49. Social media identity

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile image** | R2 mark, `--waraq` on `--lazaward`. Identical across every platform, never seasonal, never varied |
| **Banner** | Sparse pattern on `--lazaward` with the Arabic wordmark. No photography, no copy, no calls to action |
| **Post system** | Three formats only: **the statement** (one line of Source Serif on `--jiss`), **the ledger** (a table or index), **the teaching card** (a genuine short lesson, Arabic primary). No template beyond these three |
| **Never** | The seal · Qur'anic text as a graphic asset (`EB §5.1.9`) · engagement bait · countdowns · stock imagery · trend formats · memes · the mark as a watermark on Qur'anic text |

**The three-format constraint is deliberate.** Social platforms are where institutional
identities erode fastest, because the platform rewards novelty and the institution is
rewarded by consistency. A hard format limit resolves that conflict in advance, rather than
one exception at a time.

## §55. Print production

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Colour standard | **Fogra 51** (coated) / **Fogra 52** (uncoated); GRACoL 2013 in North America |
| Proofing | Contract proof on the actual specified stock. **Screen approval is never acceptable for any item carrying the seal** |
| Spot colours | `--lazaward` and `--dhahab` as spot where the run justifies it; process elsewhere |
| Overprint | Black type overprints; `--dhahab` knocks out |

| Trim tolerance | ±0.5 mm; ±0.3 mm on certificates and cards |
| Registration | ±0.1 mm where the deboss meets printed elements |
| Foil | Matte antique brass. **Gloss gold is forbidden** (§31) |
| Deboss | §22 |
| Stock — documents | 120 gsm laid, ≥25% cotton |
| Stock — certificates | 250 gsm, ≥25% cotton, unbrightened |
| Stock — covers | 300–400 gsm |
| Sustainability | FSC-certified or equivalent, recorded on the specification |

> 🔴 **Blocking production task.** Pantone values (§31) are unverified conversions. Before
> any print run: obtain a current physical swatch book, confirm each value under D50
> lighting on the actual stock, and record the confirmed values here as v1.1.

## §56. Digital production

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| Logo format | **SVG** primary, hand-optimised, no embedded raster, no unnecessary groups. PNG at 1×/2×/3× for platforms that require it |
| Fonts | WOFF2, **subset per script**, `font-display: swap`, preloaded. `font-synthesis: none` globally (`EB §15.3.3`) |
| Font budget | ≤4 files per page (`EB §24`) |
| Icons | Inline SVG sprite, `currentColor`, never an icon font (icon fonts break in Arabic reflow and fail screen readers) |

| Accessibility | axe in CI, blocking; manual screen-reader pass in **both** languages before release |

# PART IX — GOVERNANCE

## §57. Change control

| Change | Requires |
|---|---|
| New application of existing components | Design Authority sign-off |
| New component | Design Authority + documented justification against §5's three tests |
| Redrawing the mark (refinement) | Design Authority + Board note. Permitted on a ≥10-year cycle |
| **Replacing the mark or the concept** | **Board resolution recording why the concept failed.** Not a design decision |
| Palette or typeface change | Board resolution. These are `EB` amendments (`EB §47`) |
| Seal change | Board resolution + die destruction record (§22) |

**Finding 8 (§2) as policy: refine, never rebrand.**

## §58. Commissioning the calligrapher

Closes peer-review finding **B-2**.

| Stage | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Selection** | A named, practising calligrapher with a traceable training lineage (*ijāzah* in the script, where the tradition provides one). **Not a graphic designer drawing Arabic**, and not a generative model |
| **Brief** | This document's §8–§12, in full. The proportional system is not negotiable; the interpretation within it is theirs |
| **Deliverables** | Original ink drawings at large scale · vector translation · all five registers (§11) · the three seals (§19–§21) · the deboss variant (§22) |
| **Rights** | **Full assignment, worldwide, perpetual, irrevocable**, executed before work begins. No licence-back, no reserved rights |
| **Attribution** | The calligrapher is **named** in the brand manual and in the institution's published record. Attribution is owed and is not a substitute for payment |
| **Fee** | Commercial rate for institutional identity work. This is the foundational asset of a fifty-year system and is not the place to economise |

## §59. Asset library

Single source of truth. Versioned, access-controlled, institutionally owned — never a
personal account (`EB §44.2`). Holds: all mark registers; all seals including the deboss
die drawing; the wordmark originals and vectors; font files with their licences attached;
colour profiles and confirmed Pantone values; the pattern generator with its parameter set
(security parameters held separately, §22); icon library; document templates; the die
custody register.

**Nothing is recreated. Nothing is sourced from a search engine. Nothing lives on a
laptop.**

## §60. Register of rejected components

Recorded so the reasoning survives the decision, and so each argument does not have to be
re-won.

| Rejected | Ground |
|---|---|
| Coat of arms | §7 — four independent grounds |
| Crescent and star | National flag device, not religious (`EB §7.4`); fails T1 |
| Dome, minaret, mosque silhouette | Describes mosques, not colleges; fails T2 |
| Open book, lamp, graduation cap | Generic education iconography; fails T1 and T2 |
| Full-colour mark | Marks born in colour die with it (§2, finding 3) |
| Gradient, glassmorphism, blur, mesh | Dateable to this decade with precision |
| Geometric-sans wordmark | The clearest dateable marker of 2015–2022 (§2, counter-lesson) |
| Variable / morphing "responsive" logo | A 2015–2020 fashion; an institution has one mark |
| Animated logo reveal, video sting | Decoration; fails `EB §21.2.1` |
| Large corner radii, pill containers | Consumer-software convention (`EB §17`) |
| Kufic for institutional use | Archaising; a costume (§28.3) |
| Mascot, character system, illustrated people | `EB §5.3` |
| Gold foil seals, holograms | Certificate-mill signals; §25 |
| Landscape "diploma" format | Certificate-mill convention; §41 |
| Seasonal or campaign logo variants | Identity erosion; §49 |
| Sonic logo / audio brand | `EB §5.5` |
| Tagline locked to the mark | A tagline is copy; copy changes and marks do not |
