# Adversarial Audit — Academic & Editorial Bible v1.0

**10 August 2026 · Cited as `AUD-n`**
Conducted under Master Institutional Mandate §28, before delivery.

Sixteen reviewers, each reading only for what would fail *them*. The instruction was to
find contradictions and missing policies, not to confirm the document. **Eight material
gaps were found and eight provisions were written in response** (`AEB §70–§77`). Two of
them are serious enough that publishing the Bible without them would have been negligent.

## The three failures

### `AUD-1` — Safeguarding · raised by the Parent, the legal reviewer and the scholar

**The finding.** `AEB §1` lists *children/teenagers where appropriate* among those the
College serves, and `§23` describes school stages that are, in the ordinary case,
studied by minors. The Bible then contains **no safeguarding policy whatsoever** — no
minimum age, no rules for an adult teaching a child alone over video, no recording
policy, no parental consent, no reporting route, no vetting standard.

**Why this is a failure and not a gap.** An institution that puts an unvetted adult in a
private video call with a child, with no policy, no recording and no reporting route, has
created the exact conditions safeguarding exists to prevent. This is the single most
serious omission in the document. Publishing a Bible that markets to children while
silent on their protection would be indefensible, and the fact that it is a *distance*
institution makes it worse rather than better: there is no corridor, no open door, and no
colleague walking past.

**Response.** `AEB §70` — a full safeguarding provision, and an immediate **[Binding]**
prohibition: **no enrolment of anyone under 18 until it is operative.** The College may
not serve minors before it can protect them, whatever that costs in enrolment.

### `AUD-2` — Gender arrangements · raised by the Islamic scholar

**The finding.** For an Islamic college, arrangements for teaching between men and women
are not an afterthought — for a large part of the intended student body they determine
whether enrolment is possible at all. A Muslim woman deciding whether to study with
Al-Madeenah cannot find out, from this document, whether she will be alone on video with
a male teacher. Many will not enrol rather than find out afterwards.

**Response.** `AEB §71` — stated arrangements, and stated *before* the student has to ask.

### `AUD-3` — Teacher independence · raised by the Teacher, with the CFO concurring

**The finding.** `AEB §14` promises money never buys a grade. `§17` makes retakes free.
`§15` admits the mastery model creates revenue risk. But **nothing in the document
protects the individual assessor** who fails a student paying the highest tier, and
nothing prevents assessment outcomes from being read as performance data on the teacher.

This is the structural weak point of the entire academic model. Every promise in `§14`
and `§68` rests on an assessor's willingness to write *lam yablugh* on a paying
customer's attempt. If that assessor's own standing depends on pass rates, the promise is
decorative. The CFO's independent concern lands on the same point from the other side:
a mastery model has an obvious commercial temptation, and a policy that names the
temptation without building a wall against it has not addressed it.

**Response.** `AEB §72` — assessor independence, made structural rather than cultural.

## What the audit did not fix

Stated so it is not mistaken for completeness:

- **No scholarly review has taken place.** No qualified scholar has read the Qur'an,
  ḥadīth or fiqh provisions. `AEB §64` therefore holds all such content closed.
- **No native-speaker review of Arabic copy.** `EB §11.8`.
- **No legal review.** `§37.1` and `§63` are written conservatively *because* of that,
  not as a substitute for it.
- **The hour calibration is untested.** `AEB §20.2` derives from published research, not
  from our own completion data, which does not exist. It must be revised against the
  first cohort.
- **The financial model is not in this document.** It is the next deliverable.
